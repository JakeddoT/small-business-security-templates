Lost or stolen company laptop or phone? What to do in the first hour

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

When a work laptop or phone goes missing: find out whether it was encrypted, lock or wipe it remotely if you can, sign the user out of everything and reset their passwords, and write down what data was on it. Whether it becomes a reportable data breach usually depends on one question: was the device encrypted?

Report it fast, even if you think it'll turn up. Most of the steps below are reversible if the device is found, and every hour matters if it isn't.

First 15 minutes (the employee)

  1. Tell your manager or IT right away, by phone. Don't wait to search for it first.
  2. Note the details: when and where you last had it, whether it was locked, and whether it was signed in to email or other apps.
  3. If stolen, report it to the police and get a report number. Insurers and some regulators ask for it.

First hour (IT or the owner)

  1. Check encryption. Look in Microsoft Intune, the Entra admin center (BitLocker keys stored for the device) or your RMM. Phones with a passcode are generally encrypted by default on current iOS and Android. If you're unsure about a Windows laptop, see how BitLocker status is checked and check your records.
  2. Lock or wipe it remotely if it's managed (Intune, Google endpoint management, Apple/Android device management, or the RMM). Locking first and wiping later is reasonable if there's a real chance it'll be found.
  3. Sign the user out everywhere. Revoke sessions in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, so a stolen-but-unlocked device can't keep reading email.
  4. Reset passwords for the user's accounts, starting with email, and any passwords saved in the device's browser.
  5. Remove the device from the user's MFA methods if it was their authenticator, and set up a new method.
  6. Record what data was on it: local files, synced folders, email, customer exports, saved passwords. Ask the user specifically about downloads and desktop files.

Is it a data breach?

SituationUsual outcome
Encrypted (BitLocker, FileVault, phone with passcode), and the password or key wasn't with the deviceData generally considered secured; usually not a reportable breach. Document why.
Not encrypted, no sensitive data stored locallyLower risk; document what was checked and how you know
Not encrypted, with customer, patient, tax or payment dataPotentially reportable. Call your cyber insurer and a breach lawyer before notifying anyone
Password written on a sticky note on the laptopTreat as unencrypted

Breach notification rules depend on your state, your industry and the type of data. HIPAA, the FTC Safeguards Rule and state laws can each apply, often with deadlines. This is general information, not legal advice.

Prevent the next one

  • Turn on encryption on every laptop and confirm it's not suspended.
  • Enroll laptops and phones in device management so you can lock and wipe them.
  • Keep company data in cloud storage rather than the desktop, so a lost device doesn't mean lost work.
  • Put the reporting rule in your BYOD and remote work policy and your incident response plan.

Frequently asked questions

Is a lost company laptop a data breach?

It depends mainly on encryption and what data was on it. A properly encrypted device whose key wasn't exposed is generally not a reportable breach. An unencrypted device with customer, patient or financial data may be. Check with your insurer or a breach lawyer.

Can I remotely wipe a lost company laptop?

Yes, if it's enrolled in device management such as Microsoft Intune or an RMM tool. Without management, you can still revoke sessions and reset passwords so the device can't reach company accounts.

What should an employee do if they lose a work phone?

Report it to their manager or IT immediately, note when and where it was last seen, and file a police report if it was stolen. IT can then revoke sessions, reset passwords and wipe company data.
