Switching IT providers: an MSP offboarding and handover checklist

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

When a business changes IT provider, the handover should leave the business holding every admin account, domain, license and backup, with the old provider's access removed and their tools uninstalled. The key steps: confirm the business has its own global admin or super admin login, move domain and DNS access into an account the business owns, get the documentation and passwords handed over, confirm backups are transferred or exported, remove the old provider's remote tools and accounts, and rotate any shared passwords they knew. Do it on a planned date with both providers involved, not the day the contract ends.

This works both ways. If you're the business, use it to make sure nothing is left behind. If you're the MSP losing or gaining a client, a clean, documented handover protects your reputation and reduces liability.

Before you give notice

  • Read the contract for notice periods, handover obligations, and who owns documentation, licenses and hardware (see the managed IT SLA template for what good terms look like).
  • Make sure the business has its own admin login to Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, with MFA, that the old provider doesn't control. Do this first.
  • List everything the provider manages, using your asset inventory: devices, cloud services, domains, firewall, backups, security tools and line-of-business apps.

On cut-over day

  1. New provider confirms they can sign in to everything and that monitoring and backups are running.
  2. Old provider's access is removed: admin accounts, partner relationships, VPN, remote tools.
  3. Shared passwords are rotated.
  4. Staff are told who to call for help from now on, and warned that scammers sometimes pose as IT during changes (see fake IT support calls).
  5. Run a security check a week later: who's an admin, what remote tools are installed, whether backups ran (see the Windows security audit).

If the old provider won't cooperate

  • Start with the business-owned admin account. If you don't have one, Microsoft and Google both have processes to prove domain ownership and regain admin control, though they take time.
  • Your registrar can help you recover a domain if you can prove the business owns it.
  • Keep communication in writing and refer to the contract's handover terms. For serious disputes, talk to a lawyer.

For MSPs: make handovers routine

A written offboarding procedure is a sign of a mature provider. Keep client documentation in a form you can export, avoid holding the client's only admin account, and record the handover with a sign-off from the client. The same discipline makes onboarding new clients smoother (see the MSP client onboarding checklist).

Frequently asked questions

What should I get from my old IT provider when switching?

Your own admin access to Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, domain and DNS access, all passwords and documentation, your backups, and confirmation that their remote tools and accounts are removed.

How do I remove an old MSP's access?

Remove their admin accounts and partner relationships, uninstall their remote management agents, change firewall and router passwords, and rotate every shared password they knew.

Who owns the backups when I change IT provider?

It depends on the contract and on who pays for the backup service. Agree an export or transfer plan before the old service is cancelled, and have the new provider test a restore.
