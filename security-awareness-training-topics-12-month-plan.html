Security awareness training topics: a 12-month plan for small businesses

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-22

The simplest security awareness program for a small business is one short topic a month: 10–15 minutes at a team meeting, one rule to remember, and a line in your training log. Over a year that covers phishing, passwords and MFA, payment scams, devices, reporting, and the newer tricks like QR codes, fake IT support and deepfake voices. Below is a ready-made 12-month plan you can use as is, reorder, or hand to your IT provider.

Short, regular sessions tend to stick better than one long annual course, and they give you a record to show cyber insurers and auditors who ask about training. Log each session with the date and attendees in your training log.

How to run a 15-minute session

  1. Open with a real example (2 minutes): a scam email someone received, a news story, or the example on the linked page.
  2. Walk through the red flags (5 minutes): what gave it away, and what a busy person might miss.
  3. State the one rule (2 minutes) and put it on a sticky note, poster or the team chat.
  4. Ask a "what would you do?" question (5 minutes), for example: "The owner texts from a new number asking for gift cards. What now?"
  5. Log it (1 minute): date, topic, who attended (see the training log template).

Make it stick

  • The owner goes first. When the owner says "I'll never ask you for gift cards or codes, and I want you to check if anyone does," staff believe it.
  • Praise reporting. Thank people who report suspicious messages, including false alarms. Blame makes people hide mistakes.
  • Cover new starters with the essentials in their first week (see the onboarding checklist), then they join the monthly rotation.
  • Repeat the top three (phishing, payment verification, MFA codes) every year; scams change, the rules don't.
  • Test once or twice a year with a tabletop exercise for managers.

Frequently asked questions

What topics should small business security awareness training cover?

Phishing, passwords and password managers, MFA code requests, gift card and impersonation scams, payment and bank detail changes, text message scams, fake IT support, device security, QR code phishing, deepfakes, sending data safely and how to report incidents.

How often should employees get security awareness training?

Short sessions regularly work well, such as 10 to 15 minutes once a month, plus essentials in the first week for new starters. Log each session for insurers and auditors.

Do I need paid software for security awareness training?

Not to start. A monthly 15-minute talk with a clear rule and a training log covers the basics. Paid platforms add phishing simulations and tracking if you need them.
