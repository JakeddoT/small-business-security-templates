FTC Safeguards Rule checklist for small businesses

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

The FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314) requires covered non-bank financial businesses, such as tax preparers, mortgage brokers, auto dealers that arrange financing and collection agencies, to run a written information security program. That means a Qualified Individual, a risk assessment, specific safeguards (including MFA and encryption), testing, staff training, vendor oversight, an incident response plan, and FTC breach notification for events involving 500 or more people.

Many small firms don't realize they're covered until a client, lender or insurer asks. This page explains who's in scope and turns the requirements into a checklist you can work through. It's general information, not legal advice: confirm your obligations with your attorney or industry association.

Who the Safeguards Rule covers

It applies to businesses that are "financial institutions" under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and fall under FTC jurisdiction rather than a banking regulator. Common small-business examples include:

  • Tax preparers and many accounting firms (see the WISP guide for tax preparers)
  • Mortgage brokers and non-bank lenders
  • Motor vehicle dealers that arrange financing or leasing
  • Payday lenders, check cashers and money transmitters
  • Collection agencies and credit counselors
  • Some investment advisers that are not registered with the SEC

If you collect customer financial information to provide a financial product or service, assume you might be covered and check.

The under-5,000-consumer exemption

If you maintain customer information on fewer than 5,000 consumers, you're exempt from four documentation-heavy items: the written risk assessment, continuous monitoring or annual penetration testing and six-monthly vulnerability assessments, the written incident response plan, and the annual written report. The other safeguards still apply, including the Qualified Individual, MFA, encryption, training and vendor oversight.

Many small firms write the exempt documents anyway. They're short, they're what an insurer or a lawyer asks for after an incident, and you'll need them the year you pass 5,000.

Breach notification to the FTC

Since May 2024, covered businesses must notify the FTC as soon as possible, and no later than 30 days after discovery, of a "notification event" involving the unencrypted information of at least 500 consumers. The notice goes through the FTC's online form. State breach notification laws may also apply, and tax preparers should contact their IRS Stakeholder Liaison too (see tax preparer data breach steps).

A 30-day plan for a small firm

  1. Week 1: name the Qualified Individual, list where customer data lives, and turn on MFA everywhere it's missing (MFA rollout plan).
  2. Week 2: check encryption on every laptop (BitLocker check), confirm backups are encrypted and tested, and shred or securely delete old files past your retention period.
  3. Week 3: write the risk assessment and incident response plan, and list your vendors with their security contacts.
  4. Week 4: run a 30-minute staff training session, log it, and put the annual review and report date in the calendar.

Frequently asked questions

Who has to comply with the FTC Safeguards Rule?

Non-bank financial institutions under FTC jurisdiction, such as tax preparers, mortgage brokers, auto dealers that arrange financing, payday lenders, and collection agencies.

Is there a small business exemption from the Safeguards Rule?

Businesses with customer information on fewer than 5,000 consumers are exempt from some written documentation and testing requirements, but not from the core safeguards.

Does the Safeguards Rule require MFA?

Yes. It requires multi-factor authentication for any individual accessing any information system, unless the qualified individual approves an equivalent control in writing.

What happens if you don't comply with the Safeguards Rule?

The FTC can bring enforcement actions. Non-compliance can also hurt you with lenders, partners, and cyber insurers after an incident.

How often does the Safeguards Rule require penetration testing?

Unless you have continuous monitoring, at least an annual penetration test and vulnerability assessments at least every six months. Firms with customer information on fewer than 5,000 consumers are exempt from this requirement.

When must a business notify the FTC of a breach under the Safeguards Rule?

As soon as possible and no later than 30 days after discovering a notification event involving the unencrypted information of at least 500 consumers.
