Microsoft 365 email account hacked? The 8-step cleanup checklist

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

Changing the password isn't enough. When a Microsoft 365 mailbox is taken over, attackers usually leave something behind: an inbox rule that hides replies, a forwarding address, their own MFA method or an app with access. Work through all eight steps below, in order, the same day.

The signs that send most small businesses here: customers asking about an email you didn't send, replies you never received, a vendor saying "we already paid that invoice", or a sign-in alert from another country.

The 8 steps

  1. Reset the password and sign out everywhere. In the Microsoft 365 admin center, go to Users → Active users, select the user and reset the password. Then sign the user out of all sessions (also available as Revoke sessions in the Microsoft Entra admin center). Without this, existing sessions can keep working for a while.
  2. Check MFA methods. In the Entra admin center, open the user's Authentication methods. Remove any phone number or authenticator app the user doesn't recognize. Attackers add their own so they can get back in after a reset. Require re-registration if in doubt.
  3. Find malicious inbox rules. Sign in to the mailbox in Outlook on the web, open Settings, search for "rules" and open Inbox rules. Delete anything the user didn't create. Classic attacker rules move or delete messages containing words like invoice, payment, wire, bank or a specific person's name, or move everything into RSS Subscriptions, Notes or Junk where nobody looks.
  4. Remove forwarding. In Outlook on the web, check Settings → Mail → Forwarding. As an admin, also check the user's Mail tab in Active users for email forwarding. Turn off any forwarding to an address you don't control.
  5. Review sign-ins. In the Entra admin center sign-in logs, filter to the user and look at IP addresses, locations and times since the suspected start. Note the first suspicious sign-in. Your insurer or IT provider will ask for it.
  6. Check app access and delegates. Look for third-party apps the user consented to recently (Entra admin center → Enterprise applications) and for mailbox delegates or "Send as" permissions you didn't set up. Remove anything unknown.
  7. Check what was sent. Look in Sent Items and Deleted Items for phishing emails sent from the account, and for any message asking someone to pay or change bank details. Warn the people who received them, by phone for anyone who might pay an invoice.
  8. Look for money and data. Ask accounts payable and the user's contacts whether any payment was made or payment details changed on instructions from this mailbox. If so, follow the bank-change fraud first-hour steps now. Note what sensitive data was in the mailbox, because that drives whether you have notification duties.

When to call for help

  • Money was sent, or payment details were changed: call your bank now, then your cyber insurer.
  • The mailbox held health, financial or ID data for customers: talk to your insurer's breach lawyer before notifying anyone. Notification rules depend on your state and industry.
  • An admin account was compromised, or more than one mailbox: treat it as a full incident and involve your IT provider or the insurer's incident response firm.

Stop it happening again

  • Enforce MFA for every user, using an authenticator app or security key, not just SMS. See which accounts insurers expect MFA on and the MFA rollout plan.
  • Block automatic forwarding to outside addresses in your outbound spam policy (Microsoft Defender portal), and confirm no one has overridden it.
  • Turn on sign-in alerts or have your IT provider review risky sign-ins weekly.
  • Train staff on phishing and keep a training log. Insurers ask for it.

Admin center menus change often. If a path above has moved, Microsoft's "Responding to a compromised email account" article on Microsoft Learn has the current steps.

Frequently asked questions

Is changing the password enough after a Microsoft 365 account is hacked?

No. Also sign the user out of all sessions, remove unknown MFA methods, delete malicious inbox rules and forwarding, and check app consents and delegates, since attackers use these to keep access.

How do I find hidden inbox rules in Microsoft 365?

In Outlook on the web, open Settings, search for rules and open Inbox rules. Look for rules that move or delete mail with words like invoice or payment, or move mail to RSS Subscriptions, Notes or Junk.

How do I check who signed in to a Microsoft 365 account?

An admin can open the Microsoft Entra admin center sign-in logs, filter to the user, and review IP addresses, locations and times.

Do I have to report a hacked business email account?

It depends on what data was in the mailbox and your state and industry rules. If customer health, financial or ID data was exposed, talk to your cyber insurer or a breach lawyer before notifying anyone.
