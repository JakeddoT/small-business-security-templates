BYOD and remote work policy template for small business

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

A BYOD (bring your own device) and remote work policy sets the minimum security a personal phone, laptop or home network must meet before it can touch company email or data, and what happens to that data when a device is lost or an employee leaves. For most small businesses, it fits on two pages.

The risk isn't abstract. A personal laptop with no password and an old operating system, a phone with company email and no screen lock, a family PC with a saved login to your accounting software: those are how small-business data ends up somewhere it shouldn't. The policy gives you a baseline to require and the right to remove company data.

Remote work requirements

  • Use the company VPN or approved secure access (with MFA) for internal systems. Never expose office PCs to the internet to reach them from home. See why exposed Remote Desktop is dangerous.
  • Home Wi-Fi uses WPA2 or WPA3 with a non-default password, and the router's admin password has been changed from the default.
  • Avoid public Wi-Fi for work unless connected through the VPN.
  • Company devices are not shared with family members or used for personal accounts.
  • Lock the screen when stepping away, and keep work conversations and screens away from shared spaces where client data could be overheard or seen.
  • Printed documents with customer data are stored securely and shredded, not put in household recycling.

Lost devices, wiping and leaving the company

  • Report a lost or stolen device with company data on it immediately, and within 24 hours at most.
  • The company may remotely remove company data from the device. With app protection or a work profile, this removes only work apps and data, not personal photos. Explain which approach you use so staff know what to expect.
  • When someone leaves, company accounts are removed from their personal devices the same day, and they confirm in writing that they've deleted any company files. Add this to your offboarding checklist.

Sample wording you can adapt

Personal devices may be used to access company email and approved applications only if they meet the minimum requirements in this policy. The employee agrees that the company may remove company accounts and data from the device if it is lost or stolen, if the employee leaves, or if the device no longer meets these requirements. The company will not access personal data on the device.

Choosing your approach

  • Email and Teams only on phones: app protection policies (Microsoft Intune, included in Microsoft 365 Business Premium) or Google Workspace's endpoint management let you protect and wipe work data without managing the whole phone.
  • Customer, health or payment data: consider allowing that only on company-owned devices. It's cheaper than a breach. Medical and dental offices should check how BYOD fits their HIPAA risk assessment.
  • Contractors: the same rules apply, plus removal of access at contract end.

Privacy and monitoring laws vary by state and country, so have the wiping and monitoring language reviewed before you rely on it.

Frequently asked questions

Can a company wipe an employee's personal phone?

Policies typically allow wiping company data or managed apps only. Selective wipe is preferred over full device wipe; state this clearly in the policy.

Is a VPN required for remote work?

For access to internal systems it is strongly recommended; for cloud apps with MFA it may not be necessary.

What is a BYOD policy?

A bring-your-own-device policy sets the minimum security requirements for personal phones and laptops used for work, and explains how company data is protected and removed if the device is lost or the employee leaves.

What should a remote work security policy include?

Secure access with MFA, home Wi-Fi standards, rules for public Wi-Fi, device sharing, screen locking, handling printed data, and how to report lost devices or incidents.
