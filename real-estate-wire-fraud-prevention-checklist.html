Real estate wire fraud prevention checklist for agents and title offices

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

Real estate wire fraud happens when a criminal who has watched a transaction, usually through a hacked agent, title or client email account, sends the buyer fake wiring instructions just before closing. To prevent it: tell clients in writing at the start that you will never change wiring instructions by email, have the buyer confirm instructions by calling the title or escrow company on a number from an independent source, turn on MFA on every email account in the transaction, and act within minutes if money goes to the wrong account: call the sending bank and file a report at ic3.gov.

The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) has repeatedly warned about business email compromise in real estate transactions, and the losses are often a buyer's entire down payment. Small brokerages and title offices are targeted because one compromised mailbox can expose many closings at once.

How the scam usually works

  1. An email account is compromised, often an agent's, a title officer's or a buyer's, through a phishing link or reused password.
  2. The criminal reads quietly, learning names, the property, the closing date and the amount.
  3. Just before closing, they send "updated" wiring instructions from the real account or a look-alike address, often with urgency ("the account changed, please wire today").
  4. The buyer wires the funds to an account the criminal controls, and the money is moved on quickly.

Prevention checklist for the brokerage or title office

CheckDone?
MFA on every staff email account, including agents who use personal or independent mailboxes for business (see the MFA rollout plan)
A written wire fraud warning given to every client at the start of the transaction, and repeated near closing
Wiring instructions sent only through a secure portal or method agreed with the title or escrow company, never as a plain email attachment you created
A rule that any change to wiring instructions is treated as fraud until confirmed by phone on a known number
Email rules and forwarding checked regularly for unknown rules that hide or forward mail (see the Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace checklists)

A client warning you can adapt

Wire fraud is common in real estate. We will never send you new or changed wiring instructions by email, text or chat. Before you send any money, call the title or escrow company using a phone number you found yourself, such as from their official website or your closing documents, not from an email, and confirm the account details. If anything seems different or urgent, stop and call us.

Put it in your welcome email, your buyer packet and your email signature, and say it out loud at the first meeting. Clients remember a conversation better than a footer.

The call-back rule

  • The person sending the money calls the title or escrow company on a number from an independent source, never a number in the email with the instructions.
  • They confirm the account name, bank and the last digits of the account number, and ideally make a small test transfer if the title company supports that.
  • Agents should never relay wiring instructions themselves. Point the client to the title or escrow company.
  • The same rule works for supplier and vendor payments in your own office (see bank detail change verification).

If money was wired to the wrong account: the first hour

  1. Call the sending bank's fraud line immediately and ask them to recall the wire. Speed matters more than anything else.
  2. File a complaint at ic3.gov with the transaction details, and contact your local FBI field office.
  3. Tell the title or escrow company and the other agents in the deal, using phone numbers you already have.
  4. Secure email accounts: change passwords from a trusted device, sign out all sessions, and check for forwarding and inbox rules.
  5. Preserve the emails, including full headers. Don't delete anything.
  6. Tell your errors and omissions or cyber insurer, and consider talking to a lawyer about any notification obligations.

This page is general information, not legal advice. State real estate commissions, title underwriters and your brokerage may have their own requirements.

Frequently asked questions

How do you prevent wire fraud in real estate?

Warn clients in writing that wiring instructions will never change by email, have buyers confirm instructions by calling the title company on an independently found number, and use MFA on every email account involved.

What should a buyer do if they wired money to a scammer?

Call the sending bank's fraud line immediately to request a recall, file a complaint at ic3.gov, and contact the title company and agent by phone. The faster the report, the better the chance of recovery.

Should real estate agents send wiring instructions?

Generally no. Agents should direct clients to the title or escrow company and tell them to confirm any instructions by phone on a known number.
