Free small business cybersecurity self-assessment

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

Twenty yes/no questions based on what cyber insurers, clients, and payment processors most often ask small businesses about. It takes about 5 minutes. Accounts and MFA

1. Is multi-factor authentication (MFA) turned on for all email accounts?

2. Is MFA required for remote access (VPN, remote desktop, remote support tools)?

3. Do staff use normal (non-admin) accounts for everyday work?

4. Are shared passwords kept in a password manager rather than a spreadsheet or notebook?

5. When someone leaves, are all of their accounts disabled on their last day?

Devices

6. Do all computers get operating system and software updates automatically?

7. Does every computer have endpoint protection (antivirus/EDR) that is running and up to date?

8. Are laptops encrypted (BitLocker or FileVault)?

9. Do you have a current list of every computer, phone, and server the business uses?

Backups

10. Is at least one backup copy offline or immutable (ransomware cannot delete it)?

11. Have you successfully test-restored data in the last 3 months?

People

12. Have all staff had security awareness or phishing training in the last 12 months?

13. Do staff know exactly who to tell, and how, if they click a suspicious link?

14. Is there a rule to verify payment or bank-detail changes by phone using a known number?

Policies and plans

15. Do you have a written incident response plan with contacts and first steps?

16. Do you have written security policies (acceptable use, passwords/MFA, remote work)?

17. Have you walked through a ransomware or breach scenario as a team in the last year?

Vendors and payments

18. Do you know which vendors hold your customer or employee data?

19. If you take cards, do you check card terminals for tampering and follow your PCI requirements?

20. Do you know which security controls your cyber insurance policy requires?

How the score works

Each question is weighted by how often the control shows up in insurance questionnaires and real incidents. MFA on email and offline backups carry the most weight. "Not sure" counts as no, because an attacker will find out before you do.

Frequently asked questions

Is this cybersecurity self-assessment really free?

Yes. It runs in your browser, needs no sign-up, and your answers are never sent anywhere.

Who is this assessment for?

Small businesses with roughly 1 to 50 staff, including offices, retail shops, medical and dental practices, and the MSPs and IT consultants who support them.

Does a high score mean I am compliant?

No. It measures common baseline controls. It does not certify compliance with HIPAA, PCI DSS, the FTC Safeguards Rule, or any insurance policy.
