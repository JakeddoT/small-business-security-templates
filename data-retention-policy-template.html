Data retention policy template for small business

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

Keeping everything forever increases what can be stolen. A simple classification scheme and retention schedule reduces risk and makes requests from auditors easier.

Four classification levels

  • Public: approved for release, such as marketing material.
  • Internal: general business info, such as schedules and policies.
  • Confidential: contracts, customer lists, financials. Need-to-know access.
  • Restricted: payment card data, Social Security numbers, health data. Encrypted and access logged.

Example retention schedule (confirm with your accountant and counsel)

  • Financial and tax records: often 7 years
  • Employment records: often several years after separation
  • Customer contracts: term of the contract plus a set period
  • Security logs: 12 months is common
  • Backups: 30 to 90 days rolling

Disposal

Securely wipe devices and shred paper once retention ends, unless records are under legal hold.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a small business keep records?

It depends on record type and jurisdiction; tax records are often kept around 7 years. Confirm with your accountant or counsel.

What is a legal hold?

An instruction to preserve records relevant to litigation or investigation, overriding normal deletion schedules.
