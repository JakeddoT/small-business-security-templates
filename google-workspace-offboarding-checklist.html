Google Workspace offboarding checklist

By Jake Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

Suspending a Google Workspace user blocks new sign-ins, but it doesn't clean up everything they can still reach. Third-party apps, mobile devices, forwarding rules and files they own all need attention. Work through this list on their last day, in order.

On the last day (in this order)

  1. Reset their password in the Admin console (Directory › Users › select user › Reset password).
  2. Reset sign-in cookies (on the user's page, Security › Sign-in cookies › Reset). This signs them out of Google on all devices and browsers. If you use a third-party SSO provider, end their SSO session first.
  3. Revoke app access: under Security, remove app-specific passwords and revoke connected third-party applications (OAuth tokens).
  4. Remove 2-Step Verification backup codes and security keys so they can't be used for recovery.
  5. Wipe or remove mobile devices. Under Devices › Mobile and endpoints, use Account wipe to remove company data from personal phones, or Device wipe for company-owned ones.
  6. Check Gmail forwarding, delegates and filters. Remove forwarding to outside addresses and any filters that delete or redirect mail.
  7. Suspend the account once you have what you need. Suspended users can't sign in, and suspension also resets their sign-in cookies, but their data is kept.
  8. Remove them from groups and shared drives where they're a manager.

Keep their email and files

  • Transfer Drive ownership to their manager. When you delete the user, the Admin console offers a data transfer; you can also use the transfer ownership tool first.
  • Keep mail flowing by adding their address as an alias on the manager's account after deletion, or use a Google Group.
  • If you use Vault, confirm retention covers their data before deleting the account.

Within the first week

  • Suspended users are billed the same as active users. Delete the account once the data is transferred, or assign an Archived User license if you need to keep their data in Vault. On an Annual plan, deleting a user doesn't lower the bill until your renewal date.
  • Review the audit log for sign-in attempts after the suspension date.
  • Rotate shared passwords they knew: admin, banking, vendor and social accounts.
  • Update ownership of any Google Sites, Forms or Apps Script projects they created.

Frequently asked questions

Does suspending a Google Workspace user sign them out?

Suspension blocks sign-in, but you should also reset sign-in cookies and revoke app passwords and OAuth tokens to cut off existing sessions and connected apps.

Do suspended Google Workspace users still cost a license?

Yes. Google bills suspended users at the same rate as active ones. Delete the user after transferring data, or use an Archived User license if you need to retain their data.

How do I keep a former employee's Google Drive files?

Transfer ownership to another user, either during account deletion or beforehand with the Admin console's transfer ownership tool.
