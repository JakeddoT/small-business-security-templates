Google Workspace offboarding checklist

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

To offboard a Google Workspace user: reset their password and sign-in cookies, revoke app passwords and connected apps, wipe company data from their devices, and remove Gmail forwarding, all on the last day. Then transfer their Drive files and calendars, keep mail if you need it, and delete or archive the account. Suspending a user blocks new sign-ins, but it doesn't clean up connected apps, phones, forwarding or the files they own.

This guide is for small businesses with 5–100 users where an office manager or IT provider runs the Google Admin console. Admin console menus change from time to time, so if a path below has moved, Google's "Delete or remove a user from your organization" help page has the current steps.

On the last day (in this order)

  1. Reset their password in the Admin console (Directory → Users → select user → Reset password).
  2. Reset sign-in cookies (on the user's page, Security → Sign-in cookies → Reset). This signs them out of Google on all devices and browsers. If you use a third-party SSO provider, end their SSO session there first.
  3. Revoke app access: under Security, remove app-specific passwords and revoke connected third-party applications (OAuth tokens).
  4. Remove 2-Step Verification backup codes and security keys so they can't be used for recovery.
  5. Wipe or remove mobile devices. Under Devices → Mobile and endpoints, use Account wipe to remove company data from personal phones, or Device wipe for company-owned ones.
  6. Check Gmail forwarding, delegates and filters. Remove forwarding to outside addresses, mailbox delegates who shouldn't keep access, and any filters that delete or redirect mail.
  7. Remove them from groups and from shared drives where they're a manager, and hand group ownership to someone else.
  8. Suspend the account once the steps above are done. Suspended users can't sign in, and their data is kept.

Stop forwarding for everyone (optional but worth it)

Automatic forwarding is on by default in Gmail. If staff don't need to forward company mail to outside addresses, turn it off: Apps → Google Workspace → Gmail → End User Access → Automatic forwarding, and clear the option that allows users to forward email to another address. You can do this for the whole organization or one organizational unit. Once it's off, existing forwarding settings stop forwarding, which closes a common way departing staff (and attackers who take over an account) quietly copy company mail. The change can take up to 24 hours to apply.

Keep their email, files and calendars

  • Drive files: transfer ownership to their manager. Super admins can do this during deletion; other admins must transfer data before deleting the user. Tick the option to include files that aren't shared with anyone, or private files may be left behind. Files in shared drives belong to the shared drive and don't need transferring.
  • Calendars: transfer secondary and group calendars they own before deleting the account. Google warns that deleting a calendar owner also deletes their secondary calendars and events, and no further transfers are possible after deletion.
  • Email: Gmail messages aren't part of the standard Drive and Calendar transfer. If someone needs the old mail, export it with Vault (if your edition includes it) or copy it to another account before you delete. To keep new mail arriving, add their address as an alias on the manager's account after deletion, or point it to a Google Group.
  • Legal hold: if there's a dispute or retention requirement, check Vault retention and holds first. Google won't let you transfer data from or delete a user who is on litigation hold until the hold is lifted, so plan the order with your lawyer.
  • Other apps: update ownership of Google Sites, Forms, Looker Studio reports and Apps Script projects they created.

Within the first week

  • Licenses: suspended users are billed the same as active users. Delete the account once the data is transferred, or assign an Archived User license if you need to keep their data in Vault. On an Annual plan, deleting a user doesn't lower the bill until your renewal date.
  • Undo window: a deleted account can be restored for up to 20 days. After that, data that only they owned and that wasn't transferred is permanently deleted.
  • Review the audit and investigation logs for sign-in attempts after the suspension date.
  • Rotate shared passwords they knew: admin, banking, payroll, domain registrar, vendor and social media accounts.

When the departure isn't friendly

Reset the password and sign-in cookies and revoke apps during the termination meeting, not after it. Collect company devices in the same meeting. If you suspect they've already taken data or set up forwarding, check the logs and keep notes before deleting anything, and get legal advice.

Frequently asked questions

Does suspending a Google Workspace user sign them out?

Suspension blocks sign-in, but you should also reset sign-in cookies and revoke app passwords and OAuth tokens to cut off existing sessions and connected apps.

Do suspended Google Workspace users still cost a license?

Yes. Google bills suspended users at the same rate as active ones. Delete the user after transferring data, or use an Archived User license if you need to retain their data.

How do I keep a former employee's Google Drive files?

Transfer ownership to another user, either during account deletion or beforehand with the Admin console's transfer ownership tool.

Can I restore a deleted Google Workspace user?

Yes, for up to 20 days after deletion. After that, data only they owned that wasn't transferred is permanently deleted.

How do I stop former employees forwarding Google Workspace email?

Remove forwarding in their Gmail settings on the last day, and consider turning off automatic forwarding in the Admin console under Gmail, End User Access, for the whole organization.
