MSP quarterly business review (QBR) template and agenda

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-22

A good QBR for a small business client takes 45 minutes and covers five things: what happened last quarter, where their security stands, the top risks, a 90-day roadmap, and budget for the next 12 months. It's the meeting that keeps clients from seeing IT as a cost, and the one where most project work and renewals get approved.

For a 10–50 user client, the owner or office manager wants plain language, a few numbers and clear decisions, not RMM dashboards. Here's an agenda and a one-page template that works.

Questions to ask the client

  • Any new hires, leavers or role changes coming up? (See the onboarding and offboarding checklists.)
  • New software, vendors or locations planned?
  • Any suspicious emails, calls or texts staff reported, or payments that nearly went wrong?
  • When is the cyber insurance renewal, and has the insurer sent new questions?
  • Are there tasks staff work around because IT is slow or awkward?
  • Who should have admin access to key accounts if the owner is unavailable?

The 20-minute version for very small clients

For a client with fewer than 10 users, skip the ticket breakdown. Cover: anything that went wrong, the six-line security scorecard, one or two recommendations with a price, and upcoming renewals. Send it as an email if they won't take a meeting, and ask for a reply to approve.

Common QBR mistakes

  • Too much data. A screenshot of an RMM dashboard means nothing to an office manager.
  • No decisions. If nothing gets approved, the meeting felt like a sales call. End every QBR with a list of what was agreed.
  • Only bad news, or only good news. Show what improved since last quarter before raising new risks.
  • Skipping it when things are quiet. Quiet quarters are when to fix the long-standing gaps.

After the meeting

Within a day, send a short email: what was approved, owners and dates, and anything still waiting on a decision. Put approved projects on the calendar and open the tickets so the next QBR starts with progress, not a reminder.

Tips

  • Send the page 2 days before so decisions can happen in the meeting.
  • Lead with their business, not your tickets.
  • Always bring a recommendation and a price. "Should we?" stalls; "We recommend X for $Y" gets decided.
  • Use the monthly summaries. Three monthly maintenance summaries give you most of the scorecard.

Frequently asked questions

What should be in an MSP quarterly business review?

A business update from the client, service results against the SLA, a security scorecard, the top risks, a 90-day roadmap with costs, and a 12-month lifecycle and budget view, ending with decisions and owners.

How long should a QBR be for a small business client?

About 45 minutes is usually enough. Send a one-page summary two days before so the meeting can focus on decisions.

What security metrics should an MSP show in a QBR?

MFA coverage, backup and restore test status, patch compliance, EDR coverage, phishing training completion and incident response plan review, with changes since the last QBR.

What metrics should an MSP show in a QBR?

MFA coverage, Microsoft Secure Score or Google security health, last restore test, patch compliance, local admin accounts, training sessions logged and hardware due for replacement, each with the trend since last quarter.

How do you run a QBR for a very small client?

Keep it to about 20 minutes or an email: what went wrong, a short security scorecard, one or two priced recommendations and upcoming renewals, then ask for approval.
