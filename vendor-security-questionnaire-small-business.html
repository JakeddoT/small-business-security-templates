Vendor security questionnaire: 15 questions to ask

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

Before a new vendor gets access to your systems or customer data, send them a short security questionnaire of about 15 questions covering data and access, security practices, and contract and continuity, then rate the vendor low, medium or high risk and keep the answers on file. Many small-business breaches start at a vendor: an IT provider's remote tool, a billing company, a hacked supplier mailbox.

Big companies send 300-question questionnaires. You don't need that. You need enough to spot the vendors who'd be a real problem, and a record that you asked, which insurers, HIPAA, the FTC Safeguards Rule and your own clients increasingly expect.

The 15 questions

Data and access

  1. What of our data will you access or store?
  2. Where is it stored, and in which country or region?
  3. Is it encrypted in transit and at rest?
  4. Will your access be limited to what you need for the service?
  5. Will you use named accounts with MFA to access our systems (no shared logins)?

Security practices

  1. Do you have SOC 2, ISO 27001 or a similar independent assessment? Can we see the summary?
  2. Do you have a written incident response plan?
  3. How quickly will you notify us of a breach affecting our data?
  4. Do you train your staff on security, including phishing?
  5. Do you use subcontractors who will touch our data? Who?

Contract and continuity

  1. Who owns the data, and how is it returned or deleted when we leave?
  2. What are your uptime and support commitments?
  3. How do you back up our data, and how fast can you restore it?
  4. Do you carry cyber insurance?
  5. Who is our security contact, and how do we reach them urgently?

Good answers vs red flags

Remote access to our systemsNamed accounts, MFA, access logged and removed at contract end"We use one shared login for all our clients"

Keep a vendor list

One spreadsheet is enough: vendor, service, data they touch, access to your systems (yes/no, how), risk rating, questionnaire date, contract or BAA date, next review. Insurers and auditors often ask for exactly this list, and the renewal checklist includes it.

Vendor fraud: the other vendor risk

Even a secure vendor can have a hacked mailbox. The most common vendor-related loss for small businesses isn't a data breach, it's a fake "we changed banks" email. Never update payment details without a callback to a known number. See how to verify vendor bank detail changes.

Frequently asked questions

Why do small businesses need vendor security reviews?

Vendors with access to your systems or data can be a path for attackers, and insurers and clients may ask how you manage that risk.

What is a SOC 2 report?

An independent audit report on a service provider's security controls, commonly requested from SaaS and IT vendors.

What questions should I ask a vendor about security?

Ask what data they'll access and where it's stored, whether it's encrypted, whether they use named accounts with MFA, whether they have an independent assessment like SOC 2, how fast they'll notify you of a breach, and how data is returned or deleted at the end.

How often should vendors be reviewed?

High-risk vendors with system access or sensitive data yearly, medium-risk vendors every two years or at renewal, and low-risk vendors when the relationship changes.
