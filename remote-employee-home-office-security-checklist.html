Remote employee home office security checklist

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-22

For staff working from home, the security basics are: a company laptop (or a personal one that meets your minimum standard) with encryption, updates and a screen lock; MFA on every work account; a home router with its default password changed and current firmware; work files kept in company cloud storage rather than on the desktop or USB sticks; no sharing the work device with family; and a known number to call if something goes wrong. Give staff a one-page checklist and have them confirm it once a year.

What the business should set up

  • Company-owned devices where possible, managed so you can push updates, check encryption and wipe them if lost. If staff use personal computers, set a written minimum standard.
  • Cloud apps with MFA instead of exposing office servers to the internet. Never open Remote Desktop directly to the internet for home workers (see checking Remote Desktop exposure).
  • A VPN or secure remote access tool if staff need office systems that aren't in the cloud, with MFA on it.
  • Backups that cover company cloud storage, so a home laptop failure loses nothing (see the backup restore test).
  • A written policy covering approved devices, where files can be stored and how to report problems (see the acceptable use policy).
  • A record of which equipment each person has at home, in your asset inventory, so you can get it back when they leave (see the offboarding checklist).

Do home workers need a VPN?

Not always. If everything staff use is a cloud service such as Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, protected by MFA and accessed over HTTPS, a VPN adds less than people think at home. A VPN matters when staff need to reach systems in the office, such as a file server or accounting software on a local machine, and on untrusted public Wi-Fi. Consumer VPN subscriptions don't replace company security controls.

If a home device is lost, stolen or acting strangely

  1. Report it to the business immediately, even out of hours.
  2. The business signs the person out of all sessions, changes their password and, for a lost device, wipes it remotely if possible.
  3. Follow the lost laptop steps or, if they clicked something, the clicked a phishing link steps.

Frequently asked questions

How do I secure an employee's home office?

Use an encrypted, updated work laptop with a screen lock, MFA on all work accounts, a home router with its default password changed and current firmware, company cloud storage for files, and no family use of the work device.

Do remote employees need a VPN?

Not always. If staff only use cloud services with MFA, a VPN adds limited protection at home. It matters when they need office systems that aren't in the cloud, and on public Wi-Fi.

Should family members use a work laptop?

No. Shared use makes it easier for malware or mistakes to affect work accounts and data. Keep the work device for work.
