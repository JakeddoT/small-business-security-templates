Clicked a phishing link? What to do in the first 15 minutes

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

If you clicked a phishing link at work: close the page and report it right away. If you typed a password, change it from a trusted device and sign out everywhere. If you opened a file, disconnect the computer from the network (but leave it on) and call IT. Speed limits the damage far more than anything else, and reporting fast is never the wrong move.

Clicking a phishing link happens to careful people. Modern phishing pages look exactly like Microsoft, Google, DocuSign or your bank. What matters is what you do in the next 15 minutes.

Which situation are you in?

What happenedRiskDo this now
Clicked, but didn't type anything or download anythingLowClose the page, report the email
Typed a password or approved an MFA promptHighChange the password now, sign out everywhere, call IT
Downloaded or opened a file, or allowed a pop-upHighDisconnect from the network, leave the PC on, call IT
Entered card or bank detailsHighCall the bank's fraud line now

If you only clicked (no password entered, nothing downloaded)

  1. Close the page.
  2. Report the email to IT or your manager (use the Report button in Outlook or Gmail if you have one) so others can be warned and the email removed from other inboxes.
  3. Watch for unusual prompts, pop-ups or browser behavior for the rest of the day and report anything odd.

If you entered a password

  1. Change that password immediately from a trusted device, and anywhere else you reused it.
  2. Sign out of all sessions for that account if the service allows it. Attackers often already have a session, so a password change alone may not kick them out.
  3. Report it to IT right away. They can check sign-in logs, remove malicious inbox rules and forwarding, and look for MFA methods the attacker added. Full admin steps: Microsoft 365 hacked mailbox checklist.
  4. Deny any MFA prompt you didn't start, and tell IT. Repeated prompts mean the attacker has the password.

If you downloaded or opened a file

  1. Disconnect the computer from the network: unplug the network cable and turn off Wi-Fi. Don't power it off; that can destroy evidence.
  2. Call IT immediately, by phone if possible.
  3. Don't try to clean it up yourself or run random removal tools. If files start getting renamed or a ransom note appears, follow the ransomware first hour.

What to tell IT (copy this)

I clicked a link in an email from [sender] at about [time]. The subject was [subject]. I [did / didn't] enter my password, [did / didn't] approve an MFA prompt, and [did / didn't] open a file. I'm using [computer name]. I've [disconnected the computer / changed my password].

For IT or the owner

  • Check sign-in logs for the account since the click, and new inbox rules or forwarding.
  • Revoke active sessions, reset credentials and review MFA methods.
  • Run an endpoint scan, and isolate the device if a file was opened.
  • Search for and remove the same email from other mailboxes, and warn staff with a screenshot.
  • If the account sent emails or invoices, warn recipients by phone, especially anyone who pays you. See bank-change fraud steps.
  • Log the incident in your incident response plan's incident log.

Make it safe to report

The biggest factor in limiting phishing damage is how fast people report. If staff fear getting in trouble, they hide clicks, and a 10-minute fix becomes a week-long incident. Say clearly, in your acceptable use policy and in training, that honest mistakes reported quickly won't be punished.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if I clicked a phishing link but didn't enter anything?

Close the page, report the email to IT, and watch for unusual activity. The risk is lower but reporting helps protect others.

Should I turn off my computer after clicking a phishing link?

If you downloaded something, disconnect from the network but leave it on so IT can investigate.

Is changing my password enough after a phishing attack?

Not always. Also sign out of all sessions, report it so IT can check for inbox rules, forwarding and new MFA methods, and change the password anywhere else you reused it.

Should I turn off my computer after opening a phishing attachment?

No. Disconnect it from the network by unplugging the cable and turning off Wi-Fi, but leave it powered on, and call IT.
