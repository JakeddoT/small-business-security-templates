Clicked a phishing link? What to do in the first 15 minutes

By Jake Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

Clicking a phishing link happens to careful people. What matters is how fast you act. Speed limits the damage far more than anything else.

If you only clicked (no password entered, nothing downloaded)

  1. Close the page.
  2. Report the email to IT or your manager so others can be warned.
  3. Watch for unusual prompts or pop-ups and report anything odd.

If you entered a password

  1. Change that password immediately from a trusted device, and anywhere you reused it.
  2. Sign out of all sessions for that account if the service allows it.
  3. Report it to IT right away; they can check for suspicious logins and mailbox rules.
  4. Watch for MFA prompts you did not trigger, and deny them.

If you downloaded or opened a file

  1. Disconnect the computer from the network (unplug the cable, turn off Wi-Fi). Do not power it off.
  2. Call IT immediately.
  3. Do not try to clean it up yourself.

For IT or the owner

  • Check sign-in logs and new inbox forwarding rules
  • Revoke active sessions and reset credentials
  • Run an endpoint scan
  • Warn staff with a copy of the email
  • Log the incident in your incident response record

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if I clicked a phishing link but didn't enter anything?

Close the page, report the email to IT, and watch for unusual activity. The risk is lower but reporting helps protect others.

Should I turn off my computer after clicking a phishing link?

If you downloaded something, disconnect from the network but leave it on so IT can investigate.
