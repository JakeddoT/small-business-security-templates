EDR vs antivirus for small business: what you actually need

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-22

Traditional antivirus mainly blocks known malicious files. EDR (endpoint detection and response) also watches what programs do on each computer, flags suspicious behavior like mass file encryption or credential theft, and lets someone isolate a PC and investigate remotely. For most small businesses today, EDR on every computer, with alerts that a person actually reviews (often through an IT provider or a managed detection and response service), is the better standard, and it's what many cyber insurers ask about.

The difference in plain English

AntivirusEDR
Main approachRecognizes and blocks known bad filesWatches behavior and records activity, plus blocking
CatchesCommon malwareAlso hands-on-keyboard attackers, misuse of legitimate tools, ransomware behavior
ResponseQuarantine a fileIsolate the device, kill processes, investigate the timeline
Who reviews alertsOften nobodyNeeds someone: you, your IT provider or an MDR service
InsuranceSometimes no longer enough on its ownCommonly asked about on applications

Attackers increasingly use built-in tools such as PowerShell, remote access software and stolen logins rather than obvious malware files (see fake IT support calls). Behavior-based detection is designed for exactly that.

EDR, MDR and "managed" options

  • EDR is the software and console.
  • MDR (managed detection and response) adds people who watch the alerts around the clock and respond, usually as a monthly per-device service.
  • Through your IT provider: many MSPs bundle EDR, sometimes with MDR, into their service.

EDR without anyone watching the alerts gives you much less. If nobody will check alerts out of hours, an MDR service or an IT provider who includes monitoring is usually worth it, because ransomware attacks often run overnight or at weekends.

Does a small business need EDR?

If you handle customer data, card payments, health or financial information, or you have cyber insurance, EDR is a sensible baseline. Microsoft Defender for Business, included in some Microsoft 365 business plans, brings EDR capabilities to many small businesses that already pay for Microsoft 365; check what your plan includes. Other well-known EDR vendors offer small-business editions too.

Checklist

CheckDone?
EDR or business antivirus on every PC, laptop and server, compared against your asset inventory
Every device reporting into the console in the last few days; missing ones investigated
Tamper protection on, so users or malware can't switch it off
Alerts go to someone who acts, including out of hours
Someone can isolate a device remotely, and knows how
Exclusions reviewed: no broad folders or whole drives excluded
Old antivirus removed so two products don't conflict
Coverage evidence saved for insurance renewal (see the renewal checklist)

On Windows, you can check Microsoft Defender's status with Get-MpComputerStatus; the Windows security audit covers it with other checks.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between EDR and antivirus?

Antivirus mainly blocks known malicious files. EDR also monitors behavior on each device, records activity, flags suspicious actions and lets someone isolate and investigate a computer remotely.

Does my small business need EDR?

If you handle customer, payment, health or financial data or have cyber insurance, EDR on every device with someone reviewing alerts is a sensible baseline. Many insurers ask about it.

What is MDR?

Managed detection and response: a service where security staff watch your EDR alerts around the clock and respond, usually priced per device per month, often through an IT provider.
