Do you need a separate backup for Microsoft 365?

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-22

For most small businesses, yes. Microsoft 365 keeps deleted items for a limited time and protects against Microsoft's own failures, but it isn't a backup you control: if a user, an attacker or a sync error deletes or encrypts data and nobody notices for a few weeks, it may be gone. A separate Microsoft 365 backup (Microsoft's own add-on or a third-party service) gives you independent, longer-term copies of Exchange mail, OneDrive, SharePoint and Teams files that you can restore on your timeline.

Microsoft describes Microsoft 365 data protection as a shared responsibility: Microsoft keeps the service running; you're responsible for your data, accounts and access. That's why insurers and auditors often ask whether cloud data is backed up, not just on-premises servers.

Exact periods and features depend on your plan and settings, and Microsoft changes them from time to time, so check Microsoft's documentation for your tenant.

When a separate backup matters most

  • Ransomware or a compromised account that deletes or encrypts mail and files, especially if found weeks later (see the ransomware prevention checklist).
  • A departed employee's data you need months later.
  • Accidental bulk deletes or a sync client that wipes a shared library.
  • A malicious admin or a compromised admin account that removes data and settings.
  • Insurance and client requirements asking for backups of all business data, including cloud.

Options include Microsoft's own Microsoft 365 Backup add-on and a range of third-party services, often bundled by IT providers. Compare current features and pricing on each provider's site.

Make sure it actually works

  1. Confirm every user and shared mailbox is included, including new ones added automatically.
  2. Do a test restore of one email and one file this month, and a larger restore once a quarter (see the backup restore test checklist).
  3. Check the failure alerts go to someone who reads them.
  4. Record the results for insurance renewal (see the renewal checklist).

Using Google Workspace instead? The same reasoning applies: built-in recovery has time limits, so consider a separate backup, and check the Google Workspace security settings checklist.

Frequently asked questions

Does Microsoft back up my Microsoft 365 data?

Microsoft keeps the service resilient and offers limited recovery of deleted items, but it isn't a backup you control with long retention. Under its shared responsibility model, protecting your data is largely your job.

How long does Microsoft 365 keep deleted emails and files?

By default, deleted mail can be recovered for about 14 days after leaving Deleted Items (extendable to 30), and OneDrive and SharePoint recycle bins keep items for up to 93 days. Check your plan and settings.

What should a Microsoft 365 backup include?

Exchange, OneDrive, SharePoint and Teams files, daily automatic backups, retention you choose, storage separate from your tenant with deletion protection, MFA on the console, granular and bulk restore, and failure alerts.
