FTC Safeguards Rule Qualified Individual: who it should be and what they do

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

The FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314) requires every covered business to designate one Qualified Individual to oversee, implement and enforce its information security program. In a small tax practice, accounting firm, auto dealer or mortgage broker, that's usually the owner, a partner or a trusted office manager, and it can also be an outside IT provider, as long as the business keeps responsibility and oversees them.

The rule doesn't require a particular certification or job title. It requires that one named person is accountable for the program and that the designation is documented, typically in your WISP (written information security program). This is general information, not legal advice; the rule text and the FTC's own guidance are the authority.

What the Qualified Individual is responsible for

The Qualified Individual oversees the program's required elements. In practice, for a small firm, that means making sure these get done and documented:

  • Risk assessment: a written assessment of risks to customer information (see the exemption below).
  • Safeguards: access controls, a data inventory, encryption of customer information in transit and at rest, MFA for anyone accessing information systems, secure disposal, change management, and activity monitoring.
  • Testing: continuous monitoring, or periodic penetration testing and vulnerability assessments.
  • Training for staff, and making sure security staff stay current (keep a training log).
  • Vendor oversight: selecting and monitoring service providers (see the vendor questionnaire).
  • Incident response plan in writing (see the incident response plan template).
  • FTC notification: since May 2024, notifying the FTC of a "notification event" involving the information of 500 or more consumers, as soon as possible and no later than 30 days after discovery.
  • Keeping the program current as the business, systems and threats change.

The annual written report

The Qualified Individual must report in writing, at least annually, to the board of directors or equivalent governing body. If there's no board, the report goes to a senior officer responsible for the information security program. It should cover:

  • the overall status of the information security program and compliance with the rule, and
  • material matters such as risk assessment results, risk management decisions, service provider arrangements, testing results, security events and management's responses, and recommended changes.

For a small firm, a two-page memo is enough. Sample outline:

SectionExample content
Program statusWISP reviewed 2026-01; all required safeguards in place except encryption on one legacy scanner PC (plan below)

The under-5,000 consumers exemption

Businesses that maintain customer information concerning fewer than 5,000 consumers are exempt from some requirements, including the written risk assessment, the written incident response plan and the Qualified Individual's annual written report, along with certain testing requirements. Check 16 CFR 314.6 for the exact list. They're not exempt from the rest, including having an information security program and designating a Qualified Individual.

Even if you're under the threshold, writing the risk assessment and incident response plan is cheap and helps with cyber insurance applications. And count carefully: consumers whose records you still hold count, not just this year's clients.

Document the designation

[Name], [title], is designated as the Qualified Individual responsible for overseeing, implementing and enforcing [Business]'s information security program under the FTC Safeguards Rule, effective [date]. [If a service provider:] [Business] retains responsibility for compliance. [Name of senior staff member] directs and oversees [Provider]'s performance of this role.

Put that in your WISP and have the owner sign it. Tax professionals should also see the WISP guide for tax preparers and the FTC Safeguards Rule checklist.

Frequently asked questions

Who can be the Qualified Individual under the FTC Safeguards Rule?

An employee, an employee of an affiliate, or a service provider. If a service provider fills the role, the business keeps responsibility for compliance, must have a senior staff member oversee the provider, and must require the provider to maintain a compliant program.

Does the Qualified Individual need a certification?

The rule doesn't require a specific certification or title. The person needs to be able to oversee, implement and enforce the information security program.

How often does the Qualified Individual have to report?

In writing, at least annually, to the board of directors or equivalent body, or to a senior officer if there's no board. Businesses with fewer than 5,000 consumers are exempt from this annual report requirement.

Does a small tax practice need a Qualified Individual?

Tax preparers are generally covered by the Safeguards Rule, and the under-5,000-consumer exemption does not remove the requirement to designate a Qualified Individual.
