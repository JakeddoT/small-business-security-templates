The 10 red flags

#Red flagExample
1Urgency or pressure"Pay today", "account suspended", "reply in 1 hour"
2Sender doesn't quite matchacme-supply.co instead of acmesupply.com, or a Reply-To you don't recognize
3Changed bank detailsAny request to pay a new account. Call a number you already have (how to verify)
4Unexpected sign-in pageA link that asks for your Microsoft, Google or bank password
5MFA prompt you didn't startDeny it and report it: someone has your password
6Surprise attachmentInvoices, voicemails or "scanned documents" you weren't expecting
7Gift cards or secrecy"Buy gift cards for a client, keep this between us"
8Too good or too scaryRefunds, prizes, legal threats, tax problems
9Links that don't matchHover first: the real address differs from the text
10Odd tone or timingYour boss emailing at 2 a.m. from a phone, writing unlike themselves

Clicked something? Do this now

  • Close the page. Don't reply.
  • Entered a password? Change it now and sign out everywhere.
  • Opened a file? Unplug the network cable or turn off Wi-Fi, and leave the PC on.
  • Tell IT or your manager right away. Fast reporting is never the wrong move.

Using it in training

  • 10-minute huddle: walk through the poster with a real phishing email your business received (with personal details removed), and ask staff to spot the red flags.
  • New hires: include the poster with the acceptable use policy they sign.
  • Record it: log the session in your security awareness training log. Insurers ask for proof of training.

Frequently asked questions

Is the phishing poster free to use?

Yes. You can print it, share it and use it in staff training, newsletters or client handouts at no cost and without signing up.

What are the most common signs of a phishing email?

Urgency, a sender address that doesn't quite match, changed bank details, unexpected sign-in pages or attachments, MFA prompts you didn't start, requests for gift cards or secrecy, and links whose real address differs from the text.

What should an employee do after clicking a phishing link?

Close the page and report it right away. If they entered a password, change it and sign out of all sessions. If they opened a file, disconnect the computer from the network, leave it on and call IT.
