How to check a POS card reader for skimmers

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

A short inspection at the start of every shift is one of the most effective defenses a small store or restaurant has against card skimming.

  1. Know what normal looks like. Keep photos of each reader and PIN pad from every side at the register.
  2. Check the card slot and tap area. Look for anything loose, bulky, glued, or a different color. Gently tug; overlays often move.
  3. Check the PIN pad. Spongy or raised keys, or tiny holes or cameras above the keypad.
  4. Check cables and seals. New cables, extra devices, or broken tamper seals.
  5. Match the serial number to your inventory. Swapped devices are a known attack.
  6. Verify visitors. Confirm any technician with your manager or POS provider before they touch a terminal.
  7. If something is wrong, stop using the terminal, note what you saw, and call your manager and payment processor.

Log each check with date and initials. A simple log shows your processor and insurer that you take it seriously.

Frequently asked questions

How often should POS terminals be checked for skimmers?

At the start of every shift, or at least daily, with a more thorough weekly inspection.

What should I do if I find a skimmer?

Stop using the terminal, do not remove the device yourself, document it, and contact your manager, payment processor, and local police.
