Ransomware prevention checklist for small business

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-22

Most small-business ransomware starts one of a few ways: a stolen or guessed password on remote access or email, a phishing email, an unpatched internet-facing device, or a fake IT support call. The prevention basics are MFA everywhere, no Remote Desktop exposed to the internet, prompt patching of firewalls, VPNs and PCs, EDR that someone monitors, no everyday admin rights, and backups the attacker can't reach or delete, tested by restoring. The checklist below covers the twelve controls that matter most, and they're largely the same ones cyber insurers ask about.

Why backups alone aren't enough

Attackers now commonly steal data before encrypting it, then threaten to publish it. Good backups get you running again, but they don't stop the data leak, which can bring notification duties and customer fallout. That's why the controls that keep attackers out (1 to 6) matter as much as the ones that help you recover (7 and 8).

Make backups hard to destroy

  • Separate credentials: the backup system shouldn't share the same admin login as your Windows domain or Microsoft 365.
  • MFA on the backup console and on the cloud backup account.
  • An offline or immutable copy that can't be changed or deleted for a set period, even by an admin.
  • Cloud data too: Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace data benefits from a separate backup; built-in retention isn't the same as a backup you control.
  • Test it: a backup you haven't restored from is a hope, not a plan.

Where to start if you can only do three things this month

  1. Turn on MFA for email and any remote access.
  2. Close any Remote Desktop exposed to the internet.
  3. Confirm you have one backup copy that's offline or immutable, and restore a file from it.

Those three are inexpensive and address some of the most common ways small businesses are hit. Then work down the rest of the list.

Ransomware and cyber insurance

Insurance applications ask about most of these controls, and a gap can mean higher premiums, ransomware sub-limits or a declined renewal. Frequently asked questions

How do small businesses prevent ransomware?

Use MFA on email and remote access, don't expose Remote Desktop to the internet, patch firewalls, VPNs and PCs promptly, run monitored EDR, remove everyday admin rights, and keep an offline or immutable backup you've tested.

Are backups enough to protect against ransomware?

No. Backups help you recover, but many attackers steal data before encrypting it and threaten to publish it. Preventing access in the first place matters just as much.

What are the first three ransomware protections to put in place?

MFA on email and remote access, closing any internet-exposed Remote Desktop, and one offline or immutable backup copy that you've restored from.
