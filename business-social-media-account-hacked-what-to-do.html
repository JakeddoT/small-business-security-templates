Business social media account hacked: what to do

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-22

If your business Facebook page, Instagram or other social account has been taken over: try to recover it through the platform's official recovery page (for Meta, facebook.com/hacked and instagram.com/hacked), secure the email account linked to it first, remove unknown people from your Meta business settings, check for ad spend on your payment method, and warn customers through your other channels not to click links or send money. Then turn on two-factor authentication for every admin and keep at least two admins.

Business social accounts are usually taken over through a phishing message pretending to be the platform ("your page will be deleted for a copyright violation, appeal here"), a stolen password reused elsewhere, or a personal account of one admin being hacked. Scammers then run ads on your card, post scams to your followers, or demand money to give the account back.

The first hour

  1. Secure the email first. If the email address on the account is compromised, change its password and turn on MFA before anything else (see the Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace checklists).
  2. Use the official recovery page. For Facebook use facebook.com/hacked and for Instagram use instagram.com/hacked. Type the address yourself; don't use links in messages, and ignore anyone offering to "recover" your account for a fee.
  3. Check every admin's personal account. Business pages are often hijacked through one admin's personal profile. Each admin should change their password, check logged-in sessions and turn on two-factor authentication.
  4. Remove unknown people and partners from your business portfolio (Meta Business Suite or business settings), page roles and any connected ad accounts.
  5. Check payments. Look at ad accounts for campaigns you didn't create, and contact your bank or card issuer about unauthorized charges. Remove or freeze the payment method if needed.
  6. Warn customers through your website, email list, other social accounts or Google Business Profile: don't click links or send money because of messages from the hacked account.
  7. Keep a record of what happened, with screenshots and dates, in your incident log.

Scams to watch for during recovery

  • "Recovery experts" who message or comment offering to get your account back for a fee. Platforms don't use third parties for this.
  • Fake platform support messages with appeal links. Meta and others don't ask for your password or codes in direct messages.
  • Ransom demands from the hacker. Paying rarely gets the account back and marks you as a target.

Prevent it happening again

CheckDone?
Two-factor authentication on for every admin and on the linked email account, using an authenticator app or security key rather than SMS where possible (see the MFA rollout plan)
At least two trusted admins, so one compromised or departed person doesn't lock you out
The account's login email is a business address the company controls, not a former employee's personal email
Unique passwords stored in the company password manager
Agencies and freelancers given partner or limited access, not your own login, and removed when the work ends
Staff who post for the business know the "page violation, appeal here" message is a common scam (see the phishing red flags poster)
Social accounts listed in your asset inventory with who has admin access
Admin access removed on a leaver's last day (see the offboarding checklist)

If you can't recover it

  • Keep using the platform's official recovery and business support routes; if you advertise with the platform, business support channels may be available.
  • Report the account as impersonating your business if the hacker renames or misuses it, and ask customers to report it too.
  • Create a temporary replacement account and tell customers it's the official one, using your website and email list.
  • If customers lost money to scam posts from your account, tell them to contact their bank, and consider reporting at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Platform menus and recovery processes change often. Follow the current instructions on the platform's own help pages.

Frequently asked questions

What do I do if my business Facebook page was hacked?

Secure the linked email account, use facebook.com/hacked to recover access, have every admin secure their personal account, remove unknown people from your business settings, check for unauthorized ad spend and warn customers through other channels.

How do hackers take over business Instagram or Facebook accounts?

Usually through fake 'page violation' or copyright appeal messages that steal logins, reused passwords, or by hacking one admin's personal account.

Should I pay someone who offers to recover my hacked account?

No. Platforms don't use paid third parties for account recovery, and these offers are usually scams. Use the platform's official recovery pages.
