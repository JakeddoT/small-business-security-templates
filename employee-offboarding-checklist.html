Employee offboarding security checklist for small business

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

When an employee leaves, disable their accounts on the last day, revoke their sessions and MFA devices, recover company hardware, and reset every shared password they knew. That last step, resetting the shared Wi-Fi key, the front-desk login, vendor portals and alarm codes, is the one small businesses miss most often, and it's how former staff (or whoever ends up with their old notes) keep access for months.

Most offboarding problems aren't malicious. They're a former employee's phone still syncing email, a paid license nobody removed, or a vendor portal that still sends invoices to an ex-employee's inbox. Use the checklist below for every departure, voluntary or not.

Before the last day (planned departures)

  • Get a list from the manager of everything the person has access to, including vendor portals and social media.
  • Identify files, mailboxes and calendars that need a new owner.
  • Agree on the cut-off time with HR. For involuntary departures, accounts are disabled during the exit meeting, not after.

After the last day

  • Review sign-in and account activity for the final 30 days, especially downloads, forwarding rules and external sharing.
  • Preserve data, then wipe or reimage returned devices before reissuing them.
  • Remove or reassign paid licenses (a common, quiet monthly cost).
  • Archive or delete the account per your retention policy, typically after 30–90 days.
  • Update vendor contacts so invoices and alerts don't go to the old mailbox.
  • Log completion with the date and who did it.

Platform-specific steps

  • Microsoft 365: block sign-in, revoke sessions, convert the mailbox to a shared mailbox if needed. Full steps: Microsoft 365 offboarding checklist.
  • Google Workspace: suspend the user, transfer Drive and Calendar data, then delete or keep as archived. Full steps: Google Workspace offboarding checklist.
  • Windows PCs: confirm encryption is on before reissuing. See the BitLocker check.

The shared-password list (build it once)

Most businesses can't reset shared passwords quickly because nobody knows which ones exist. Build the list once and keep it in your password manager:

Shared credentialWho knows itHow to change it

Every credential you convert from shared to named-user removes a line from this list for good.

Frequently asked questions

When should an employee's access be removed?

By the end of their last working day, or immediately for involuntary terminations.

What is most often missed when offboarding employees?

Shared passwords, active sessions on phones and browsers, and SaaS apps outside the main directory.

What is the most commonly missed offboarding step?

Resetting shared passwords the departing employee knew: Wi-Fi keys, shared logins, vendor portals, alarm codes and social media accounts.

Should I delete a former employee's email account right away?

Usually not. Block sign-in on the last day, forward or delegate the mailbox, transfer files, and delete or archive the account later according to your retention policy.

How do I remove company data from an ex-employee's personal phone?

Remove the work account or use your device management's selective wipe to remove work apps and data, and ask the employee to confirm in writing that company files are deleted.
