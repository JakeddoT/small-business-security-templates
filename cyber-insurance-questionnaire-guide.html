How to answer a cyber insurance questionnaire: a small business guide

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

Answer every cyber insurance question about what's true today, not what you plan to do, and keep evidence for each "yes". Insurers rely on your application when they price the policy and when they decide a claim. A "yes" to MFA or tested backups that turns out to be wrong after an incident can give them grounds to dispute coverage.

Applications have become stricter over the last few years, and the wording varies by insurer. Below are the questions that appear on most small business applications, what they actually mean, and what evidence to keep. This is general information, not insurance advice; ask your broker about anything unclear.

Common questions and what they mean

How to handle "partly"

  • If the form allows notes, use them. "MFA enforced for all users except one scanner account, restricted to office IP" is honest and often acceptable.
  • If it's yes/no only, ask your broker how to answer, or close the gap before you sign. Most MFA gaps can be closed in a week.
  • Never answer for plans. "We're rolling it out next month" is a "no" today.

Keep an evidence folder

Make one folder per policy year with: MFA screenshots and registration report, backup reports and the restore test log, EDR or antivirus coverage, training records, signed policies, the incident response plan, and a copy of the submitted application. It makes renewals faster and supports a claim if you ever need one.

Before you sign

  1. Get last year's application from your broker and compare it to reality.
  2. Ask your IT provider to confirm technical answers in writing.
  3. Fix quick gaps first: MFA, exposed RDP, the missing restore test.
  4. Use the renewal checklist to plan the rest 60 days out.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if I answer a cyber insurance question incorrectly?

Misstatements can lead to denied claims or voided coverage. Answer accurately and talk to your broker about gaps.

What controls do cyber insurers require most often?

MFA, EDR, offline or immutable backups, patching, phishing training, and an incident response plan.

What happens if I answer a cyber insurance application incorrectly?

Insurers rely on the application when pricing and paying claims. Material misstatements can give them grounds to dispute or deny a claim, or in some cases rescind the policy, so answer accurately and keep evidence.

What is EDR on a cyber insurance application?

Endpoint detection and response: security software that detects and responds to suspicious activity on computers, usually with monitoring. It goes beyond traditional antivirus, and many insurers ask whether it's on all endpoints.

How do I answer a question when a control is only partly in place?

Use the notes field if there is one and describe the gap and any compensating control. If the form is yes/no only, ask your broker or close the gap before signing.
