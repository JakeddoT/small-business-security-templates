Microsoft Secure Score for small business: what to fix first

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-22

Microsoft Secure Score is a percentage in the Microsoft Defender portal (security.microsoft.com) that measures how many of Microsoft's recommended security settings your Microsoft 365 tenant has in place. For a small business, don't chase 100%: fix the high-impact items first, which are MFA for every user and especially admins, blocking legacy authentication, limiting the number of global admins, turning on mailbox auditing and alert policies, and protecting email against phishing and malware. Then review the score monthly.

Secure Score is a useful to-do list, not a certificate. A high score doesn't mean you can't be breached, and some recommendations depend on licenses you may not have. Cyber insurers don't usually ask for the number itself, but many of the items it checks, such as MFA and admin controls, are exactly what insurance questionnaires ask about (see the insurer MFA requirements).

Where to find it

Sign in to the Microsoft Defender portal at security.microsoft.com with an admin account and open Microsoft Secure Score. Microsoft has been moving it under Exposure management, and menus change from time to time, so if you can't see it, search for "Secure Score" in the portal. You need a suitable admin or security reader role to view it.

Before changing sign-in settings such as legacy authentication or MFA for everyone, make sure you have a working admin account with MFA and a plan for staff who will be prompted, so nobody is locked out on a Monday morning.

Recommendations you can deprioritize

  • Items that need licenses you don't have. Secure Score may suggest features from higher plans. Note them for a future license review rather than buying on the spot.
  • Items that don't fit how you work, as long as you decide deliberately. You can mark a recommendation as accepted risk or resolved through a third-party tool, with a note explaining why.
  • Low-point items that cause a lot of friction. Do them later, after the high-impact list.

A simple monthly routine

  1. Open Secure Score and note the percentage and date in your security log.
  2. Check whether any new recommendations appeared, since Microsoft adds them over time.
  3. Pick one or two to complete this month, starting with the highest impact.
  4. Review admin accounts and remove any that aren't needed.
  5. Record what changed, which also helps at insurance renewal (see the renewal checklist).

If you have an IT provider, ask them to report your Secure Score and what they changed in each quarterly review (see the QBR template).

Secure Score covers the cloud, not your PCs

The Microsoft 365 side of Secure Score is about tenant settings. Your Windows PCs need their own checks: encryption, updates, firewall, local admins and remote access. See the Windows security audit with PowerShell and checking BitLocker on every PC.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good Microsoft Secure Score for a small business?

There's no magic number. Focus on completing the high-impact items, especially MFA for all users and admins, blocking legacy authentication and limiting admin accounts, then improve steadily month by month.

Where do I find Microsoft Secure Score?

In the Microsoft Defender portal at security.microsoft.com, signed in with a suitable admin role. Microsoft has been placing it under Exposure management; search for Secure Score if the menu has moved.

Does a high Secure Score mean I'm secure?

No. It measures how many recommended settings are in place in Microsoft 365. It doesn't cover everything, such as your PCs, backups or staff behavior.
