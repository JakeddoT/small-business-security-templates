Google Workspace account hacked? The 8-step cleanup checklist

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

Resetting the password isn't enough when a Google Workspace account is taken over. Attackers commonly add a Gmail filter that hides replies, forward mail to an outside address, grant themselves delegate access or connect an app with access to the mailbox. Work through all eight steps below, in order, the same day.

Typical signs: contacts asking about an email you didn't send, missing replies, a Google security alert about a new sign-in, or a vendor saying they've already been paid.

The 8 steps

  1. Reset the password and sign out everywhere. In the Admin console, go to Directory → Users, open the user, and reset the password. In the user's Security section, reset sign-in cookies so existing sessions are signed out.
  2. Check 2-Step Verification. In the same Security section, review the user's 2-Step Verification settings and any backup codes or app passwords. Remove anything the user doesn't recognize, and revoke app passwords.
  3. Remove malicious filters. Sign in as the user (after the reset) and open Gmail → Settings → See all settings → Filters and Blocked Addresses. Delete filters the user didn't create, especially ones that delete, archive or skip the inbox for messages containing words like invoice, payment, wire or a specific contact's name.
  4. Remove forwarding. In Forwarding and POP/IMAP, remove any forwarding address the user doesn't control, and disable POP/IMAP if they don't use it. Also check filters that forward specific messages.
  5. Remove delegates. In Settings → Accounts, check "Grant access to your account" and remove any delegate you didn't set up. Check "Send mail as" for unfamiliar addresses.
  6. Review third-party app access. In the user's Security section in the Admin console, review connected applications and remove anything unfamiliar. Admins can also review app access under Security → Access and data control → API controls.
  7. Review sign-in activity. In the Admin console, use Reporting → Audit and investigation → Login log events, filtered to the user, to find the first suspicious sign-in (IP address, location, time). Record it for your insurer or IT provider.
  8. Check what was sent and paid. Look through Sent and Trash for phishing or invoices sent by the attacker, warn recipients by phone, and ask accounts payable whether any payment details were changed. If money moved, follow the bank-change fraud first-hour steps.

Menu names in the Google Admin console change from time to time. If a path above has moved, Google's admin help on a compromised account has the current steps.

When to call for help

  • Money was sent or payment details changed: bank first, then your cyber insurer.
  • The mailbox contained customer health, financial or ID data: talk to your insurer's breach lawyer before notifying anyone.
  • An admin account or several users were affected: treat it as a full incident and involve your IT provider.

Stop it happening again

  • Enforce 2-Step Verification for everyone (Security → Authentication → 2-Step Verification), preferring security keys or passkeys for admins. See which accounts insurers expect MFA on.
  • Restrict automatic forwarding to outside addresses in Gmail settings for your organization, if your business doesn't need it.
  • Limit third-party app access so users can't grant full mailbox access to unknown apps.
  • Train staff to spot fake Google sign-in pages and keep a training log.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if my Google Workspace account was hacked?

Reset the password and sign-in cookies, review 2-Step Verification and app passwords, remove unknown Gmail filters, forwarding and delegates, remove unfamiliar connected apps, review login events and check what the attacker sent.

How do I find hidden Gmail filters?

Open Gmail settings, choose See all settings, and go to Filters and Blocked Addresses. Delete any filter you didn't create, especially ones that delete or skip the inbox for invoice or payment emails.

How do I sign a Google Workspace user out of all sessions?

In the Google Admin console, open the user and reset their sign-in cookies in the Security section, which signs them out of active sessions.
