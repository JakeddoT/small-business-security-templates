Incident response plan template for small business

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

An incident response plan is a short document that tells your team exactly what to do when something goes wrong: a phishing compromise, ransomware, a lost laptop, or a data leak. Writing it before you need it is the difference between a bad day and a business-ending week.

Severity levels

  • Low: contained, no data exposure (for example, a blocked phishing email). Respond within one business day.
  • Medium: one account or device affected (for example, a compromised mailbox). Respond within four hours.
  • High: multiple systems or confirmed data exposure (for example, ransomware). Respond immediately.

The six response steps

  1. Identify: confirm what happened, when, and which systems are affected.
  2. Contain: disconnect affected devices from the network without powering them off, and disable compromised accounts.
  3. Preserve: keep logs, emails, and screenshots. Do not wipe devices yet.
  4. Eradicate: remove malware, reset credentials, and patch the weakness.
  5. Recover: restore from known-good backups and monitor for recurrence.
  6. Review: hold a lessons-learned meeting within a week and update controls.

What to include in the document

  • An incident lead and backup, with phone numbers
  • IT provider, cyber insurer, and legal counsel contacts
  • Severity definitions and response targets
  • The six steps above
  • A simple incident log: date, reporter, systems, actions, resolution

Notification rules after a data breach vary by state and industry and often have deadlines, so contact counsel early.

Frequently asked questions

What should a small business incident response plan include?

Contacts, severity levels, the steps to identify, contain, preserve evidence, eradicate, recover, and review, plus an incident log.

Should I turn off a computer infected with ransomware?

Generally disconnect it from the network but do not power it off until evidence is preserved, unless your IT provider advises otherwise.

How long should an incident response plan be?

For a small business, two to four pages is enough if it lists contacts and clear steps.
