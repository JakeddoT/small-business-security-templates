Cyber insurance renewal checklist for small business: what to prepare

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-22

Start your cyber insurance renewal 60 days early: compare last year's application to reality, close the gaps insurers care about most (MFA, backups, EDR, exposed remote access), and assemble an evidence folder before the new application arrives. Starting early is the difference between fixing a gap and having to explain it, and gaps commonly lead to higher premiums, exclusions or a declined renewal.

This is general information, not insurance advice. Your broker can tell you what your specific insurer will ask.

60 days before renewal

  • Get last year's application from your broker and re-read every answer. Mark anything that's no longer true, or never was.
  • List incidents, claims and near-misses since the last renewal (a phishing click, a lost laptop, a fraud attempt). Insurers usually ask.
  • Confirm MFA is enforced on email for every user, all remote access and admin accounts. See what insurers expect.
  • Confirm endpoint protection (and EDR if you have it) is on every device, with nothing missing from the console.
  • Check for Remote Desktop or other remote access exposed to the internet. See how to check.
  • Ask your IT provider for a list of end-of-life systems.

30 days before

  • Run and document a backup restore test (checklist and log).
  • Export a patch compliance report or run a quick baseline on each PC (PowerShell checks).
  • Collect security awareness training records for all staff.
  • Update your incident response plan and its contact list, including the insurer's breach hotline.
  • Remove, upgrade or isolate end-of-life systems, or document a plan and compensating controls.
  • Confirm you have a written callback procedure for payment and bank-detail changes (template).

When the application arrives

  1. Answer about the present, not plans. Use notes fields to explain partial controls.
  2. Have your IT provider review and confirm technical answers in writing.
  3. Compare limits, retention (deductible) and sub-limits for ransomware and funds transfer fraud with last year. A cheaper renewal sometimes hides a smaller sub-limit.
  4. Keep a copy of the submitted application in the evidence folder. For help with specific questions, see the questionnaire guide.

Gaps that commonly raise premiums or block renewal

  • MFA missing on email or any remote access path
  • Remote Desktop exposed to the internet
  • Backups reachable from the main network with the same credentials
  • No written incident response plan
  • Unsupported operating systems still in use
  • No EDR, or EDR not monitored

Questions to ask your broker

  • Has the insurer changed its minimum security requirements since last year, and are any new questions on the application?
  • Are ransomware, funds transfer fraud or social engineering subject to sub-limits or a higher retention this year?
  • Does the policy require panel vendors or consent before incurring incident costs? (See what to do when filing a claim.)
  • What conditions apply to payment fraud cover, such as a documented call-back procedure?
  • Would a supplemental application or a call with the underwriter help explain a control that's only partly in place?
  • Is business interruption covered, and what's the waiting period before it starts?

If you can't fix a gap before renewal

  1. Don't answer "yes" to a control you don't have. Inaccurate answers can create serious problems if you ever claim.
  2. Write down a dated plan to close the gap, with an owner and a target date, and say so in the notes field or a cover letter through your broker.
  3. Show compensating controls, for example remote access limited to a VPN with MFA while an old system is replaced, or an isolated network segment for an unsupported PC.
  4. Fix the cheapest high-impact gaps first. MFA on email and remote access, and taking Remote Desktop off the internet, usually cost little and matter a lot.

If you had an incident or claim this year

  • Expect questions about it. Prepare a short summary: what happened, what it cost, and what you changed afterwards.
  • Include evidence of the fixes, such as new MFA settings, a revised call-back procedure or the results of a tabletop exercise.
  • Near-misses count too. A blocked phishing attempt that led to new training shows a working program.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start preparing for cyber insurance renewal?

About 60 days before the renewal date, so there is time to close gaps.

What evidence do cyber insurers ask for?

Typically proof of MFA, backups and restore testing, endpoint protection, patching, training, and written policies.

What documents do I need for cyber insurance renewal?

Commonly: MFA screenshots, backup reports and a restore test log, EDR or antivirus coverage, patch reports, training records, signed policies, an incident response plan and a vendor list.

What makes cyber insurance premiums go up?

Common reasons include missing MFA, exposed remote desktop, backups that ransomware can reach, no incident response plan, unsupported software, past claims, and wider market changes.

What should I do before my cyber insurance renewal?

Start about 60 days early: re-read last year's application, close gaps in MFA, backups, endpoint protection and exposed remote access, run a restore test, and collect evidence in one folder.

What if I can't fix a security gap before renewal?

Answer accurately, document a dated plan to fix it, describe any compensating controls, and discuss it with your broker rather than answering yes to a control you don't have.
