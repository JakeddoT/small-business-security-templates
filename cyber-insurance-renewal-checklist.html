Cyber insurance renewal checklist for small business

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

Renewal season is when insurers ask the hardest questions. Starting 60 days early gives you time to fix gaps instead of explaining them.

60 days before renewal

  • Get last year's application from your broker and compare your answers to reality
  • List any incidents, claims, or near-misses since the last renewal
  • Confirm MFA is enforced on email, remote access, and admin accounts
  • Confirm endpoint protection is on every device

30 days before

  • Run and document a backup restore test
  • Export a patch compliance report
  • Collect training completion records
  • Update your incident response plan and contact list
  • Remove or isolate end-of-life systems

Evidence folder

  • MFA configuration screenshots
  • Backup reports and restore log
  • EDR or antivirus console screenshot showing coverage
  • Signed policies and training records
  • Vendor list for third parties with system access

Gaps that commonly raise premiums

  • MFA missing on any remote access path
  • Backups reachable from the main network
  • No written incident response plan
  • Unsupported operating systems still in use

Frequently asked questions

When should I start preparing for cyber insurance renewal?

About 60 days before the renewal date, so there is time to close gaps.

What evidence do cyber insurers ask for?

Typically proof of MFA, backups and restore testing, endpoint protection, patching, training, and written policies.
