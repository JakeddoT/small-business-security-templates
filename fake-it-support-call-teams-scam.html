Fake IT support calls and Teams messages: how the scam works and what to do

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

In a fake IT support scam, someone contacts a staff member by phone or Microsoft Teams pretending to be the help desk, often right after the person's inbox has been flooded with spam, and talks them into opening a remote access tool such as Quick Assist or entering their password and MFA code. If it's happened: disconnect the computer from the network, end the remote session, reset the person's password from another device, and call your real IT provider. The prevention is a simple rule: IT never asks you to start a remote session or read out a code from an unexpected call or chat; hang up and call IT on the number you already have.

Microsoft has reported criminal groups using exactly this approach, including impersonating IT staff through Teams and abusing the built-in Quick Assist tool, and in some cases it has led to ransomware. Small businesses that use an outside IT provider are easy targets because staff are used to hearing from someone they've never met.

How the scam usually goes

  1. The setup: the person's inbox suddenly fills with hundreds of newsletter sign-ups or spam ("email bombing").
  2. The call or chat: minutes later someone calls or sends a Teams message from an account named something like "IT Support" or "Help Desk", offering to fix the spam.
  3. The ask: they ask the person to open Quick Assist or another remote tool and share a code, or to sign in on a page they send, or to approve an MFA prompt.
  4. The payoff: with remote control or the login, they install software, steal data or move on to other computers.

The staff rule

If anyone contacts you claiming to be IT, Microsoft or a vendor's support and you didn't ask them to, don't open any remote access tool, don't share a code and don't approve a sign-in. Hang up or close the chat, and contact IT using the number or address you already have. Report it either way.

Print it next to the phishing red flags poster, cover it in onboarding (see the onboarding checklist), and agree with your IT provider how they will identify themselves, for example always from a known number or always through a ticket you raised.

If someone already gave access

  1. Disconnect the computer from the network (unplug the cable or turn off Wi-Fi). Don't turn it off unless your IT provider says to, as it may hold evidence.
  2. End the remote session and close the remote tool.
  3. Reset the person's password from a different, trusted device, sign out all sessions and check their MFA methods (see the Microsoft 365 hacked account checklist).
  4. Call your real IT provider and ask them to check the computer for anything installed and to look for activity on other machines.
  5. Watch for ransomware signs and follow your ransomware first-hour steps if files start changing.
  6. Tell your cyber insurer early if you have a policy, and record it in your incident log.

Settings that reduce the risk

  • Limit external Teams chat to the organizations you actually work with, using external access settings in the Teams admin center. Ask your IT provider to review it.
  • Remove remote access tools you don't use, including Quick Assist if your IT provider uses something else, so there's only one approved tool.
  • Remove local admin rights from everyday user accounts so a scammer can't easily install software.
  • Use phishing-resistant MFA where you can, and number matching on push approvals (see the MFA rollout plan).
  • Have EDR or business antivirus monitored by someone who will act on alerts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if an IT support call is fake?

Be suspicious of any call or Teams chat you didn't ask for that claims to be IT or Microsoft, especially after a sudden flood of spam, and that asks you to open a remote tool, share a code or approve a sign-in. Hang up and call IT on a number you already have.

What should I do if I let a fake IT person into my computer?

Disconnect the computer from the network, close the remote session, reset your password from another device, and call your real IT provider straight away.

Can I block scammers from messaging staff in Microsoft Teams?

Admins can restrict external access in the Teams admin center so staff can only chat with organizations you trust. Ask your IT provider to review the setting.
