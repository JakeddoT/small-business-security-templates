Shared email login vs shared mailbox: the safer way to share an inbox

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-22

Instead of several people signing in to info@ or orders@ with one shared password, give each person their own account and give them access to a shared mailbox (Microsoft 365) or a collaborative inbox or delegated mailbox (Google Workspace). Everyone keeps their own password and MFA, you can see who did what, and removing someone takes one click instead of changing a password everyone knows. In Microsoft 365, a shared mailbox generally doesn't need its own license, within Microsoft's size limits.

Why a shared password is a problem

  • MFA is awkward or skipped because the code goes to one person's phone, so the account often ends up with no MFA at all.
  • No accountability: you can't tell who sent, deleted or forwarded a message.
  • Leavers keep access until someone remembers to change the password, and then everyone needs the new one.
  • It's a favorite target: generic addresses are public, receive lots of mail and attract phishing (see the hacked account checklist).
  • Insurance questions about MFA on all email accounts become hard to answer honestly (see the MFA requirements).

Microsoft 365: shared mailbox

  1. In the Microsoft 365 admin center, go to Teams & groups → Shared mailboxes and add a shared mailbox (or convert an existing user mailbox).
  2. Add the members who need access. Give "Send as" or "Send on behalf" permission if they reply from the shared address.
  3. Make sure direct sign-in to the shared mailbox's own account is blocked, so it's only reached through members' accounts.
  4. If you converted a user mailbox, remove the old license after checking the mailbox size and any retention needs.
  5. Members see the shared mailbox in Outlook automatically or add it manually.

Google Workspace: collaborative inbox or delegation

  • Google Groups as a collaborative inbox: mail to orders@ goes to a group; members can view, assign and reply.
  • Mail delegation: a user can grant others access to read and send from their mailbox without sharing the password, if your admin allows it.
  • Either way, each person signs in with their own account and 2-Step Verification (see the Google Workspace settings checklist).

When someone leaves

Remove them from the shared mailbox or group, disable their own account, and you're done: no password change for everyone else (see the offboarding checklist and the Microsoft 365 offboarding checklist).

Admin center menus and licensing rules change; check Microsoft's or Google's current documentation before converting mailboxes.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to share one email password among employees?

It's risky. MFA is often skipped, you can't tell who did what, and leavers keep access until the password changes. A shared mailbox or collaborative inbox avoids all three.

Does a Microsoft 365 shared mailbox need a license?

Generally not, within Microsoft's size limits and unless you need features such as an archive or litigation hold. Check Microsoft's current licensing guidance.

How do I share an inbox in Google Workspace?

Use a Google Group as a collaborative inbox, or mail delegation if your admin allows it, so each person uses their own account and 2-Step Verification.
