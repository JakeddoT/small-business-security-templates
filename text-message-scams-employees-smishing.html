Text message scams targeting employees (smishing): what to tell staff

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-22

Smishing is phishing by text message. The versions aimed at businesses are texts that appear to come from the owner or a manager asking for a favor, texts asking an employee to send back an MFA or verification code, fake IT or Microsoft alerts with a sign-in link, and fake delivery, toll or bank texts on work phones. The staff rule: never send a code, click a sign-in link or buy anything because of a text; contact the person or company the way you normally would. Report it, and forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM) in the US.

Texts work for scammers because people read them fast, on small screens, often away from their desk, and because a text from an unknown number claiming to be "your boss's new number" is easy to believe. Work phones and personal phones used for work email are both targets.

The staff rule

Never reply to a text with a code, click a sign-in or payment link, or buy anything because of a text, even if it seems to come from the owner, IT, HR, the bank or a supplier. Contact them the way you normally do, using a number or app you already have. If you're unsure, ask, and report the text either way.

Put it next to the phishing red flags poster and add it to onboarding (see the onboarding checklist). Owners can help a lot by telling staff, "I will never text you asking for gift cards, payments or codes."

How to report a scam text

  • Tell your manager or IT so colleagues can be warned; the same text often goes to several staff.
  • Forward it to 7726 (SPAM), which most US carriers support, and use your phone's "report junk" option.
  • Report fraud to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov if someone lost money or data.
  • Then delete it and block the number.

If someone already replied or clicked

  1. Sent an MFA code or entered a password: change the password from a trusted device straight away, sign out all sessions and check MFA methods (see the Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace checklist).
  2. Bought gift cards: contact the card issuer immediately with the card numbers and receipt; it sometimes helps if done fast.
  3. Gave card or bank details: call the bank's fraud line now.
  4. Installed something: stop using the phone for work and ask your IT provider to check it.
  5. Log it in your incident log and remind staff of the rule.

Settings that reduce the damage

  • Move away from SMS codes for work accounts where you can, to an authenticator app with number matching, passkeys or security keys, which are much harder to phish (see the MFA rollout plan).
  • Protect work email on phones with a screen lock and, if possible, device management or app protection (see the BYOD policy).
  • Keep staff phone numbers off public pages where you can; scammers build target lists from websites and social profiles.

Frequently asked questions

What is smishing?

Smishing is phishing by text message: a text that tries to get you to click a link, share a code or password, or send money, often pretending to be your boss, IT, a bank or a delivery company.

Should employees ever text back an MFA code?

No. Legitimate services don't ask you to send a verification code back to anyone. A request for your code usually means someone is trying to sign in with your password.

How do I report a scam text?

Forward it to 7726 (SPAM) in the US, use your phone's report junk option, tell your manager or IT, and report fraud at ReportFraud.ftc.gov if money or data was lost.
