Sent customer data to the wrong person? What to do next

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

If you emailed sensitive data to the wrong person: tell your manager now, contact the recipient and ask them to delete it and confirm in writing, and record exactly what was sent, to whom and when. Whether you have to notify anyone depends on what data it was, who received it and the rules for your state and industry.

Misdirected email (autocomplete picking the wrong "John") is one of the most common small-business data incidents. It's usually an honest mistake, and handled quickly it's usually contained. Hidden, it can become a much bigger problem.

The first hour

  1. Tell your manager or the person who handles incidents. Don't try to fix it quietly.
  2. Try to recall the message if you use Outlook with Microsoft 365. Recall only works in limited cases (generally within your own organization), so don't rely on it for outside recipients.
  3. Contact the recipient, by phone if you can, and email too. Ask them not to open or forward it, to delete it (including from deleted items), and to confirm in writing that they've done so.
  4. Record the details: who sent it, when, to which address, which attachments, and exactly what personal data they contained.
  5. Check for forwarding. Ask the recipient whether it was forwarded or downloaded. Note their answer.

Sample deletion request

Subject: Email sent to you in error: please delete

Hello [Name], an email from [sender] at [time] today, subject "[subject]", was sent to you by mistake and contains confidential information. Please don't open the attachment, delete the email from your inbox and deleted items, and reply to confirm you've done so and haven't forwarded or saved it. Thank you, and sorry for the trouble. [Name, phone]

Does it need to be reported?

What was sentThink about
Names and contact details onlyUsually low risk; record it and the deletion confirmation
Social Security numbers, bank or card numbers, driver's license numbersOften covered by state breach notification laws; get advice before deciding
Patient health information (medical and dental practices)HIPAA presumes a breach unless a documented risk assessment shows a low probability of compromise; consider who received it and whether deletion was confirmed
Tax return or financial data (tax preparers, accountants)FTC Safeguards Rule and IRS guidance for tax professionals may apply
Sent to a trusted professional (another practice, your accountant) who confirmed deletionOften lower risk, but still record the assessment

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules and deadlines vary, so for anything beyond contact details, talk to your cyber insurer or a breach lawyer before notifying customers.

Prevent it

  • Turn on external recipient warnings (Microsoft 365 MailTips and Gmail show warnings when emailing outside your organization).
  • Clear bad autocomplete entries and teach staff to check the address before sending files.
  • Share links, not attachments, for sensitive files. A link can be revoked; an attachment can't.
  • Use encrypted email or a secure portal for health, tax and financial data.
  • Make reporting safe. Staff who fear blame delay reporting. Say so in your acceptable use policy.

Frequently asked questions

Is sending an email to the wrong person a data breach?

It can be, depending on what data was in it, who received it and the rules that apply to you. Names and emails alone are usually low risk; SSNs, financial data or health information may trigger notification duties. Record it and get advice for sensitive data.

Can I recall an email sent to the wrong person?

Outlook's recall works only in limited cases, generally only for recipients inside your own Microsoft 365 organization. For outside recipients, contact them directly and ask them to delete it.

What should I ask the wrong recipient to do?

Ask them not to open or forward it, to delete it from their inbox and deleted items, and to confirm in writing that they've deleted it and haven't saved or shared it.
