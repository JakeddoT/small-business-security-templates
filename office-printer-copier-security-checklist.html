Office printer and copier security checklist

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-22

Office printers and multifunction copiers are small computers on your network: they have admin web pages, firmware, stored scans and print jobs, address books, and often a saved email password for scan-to-email. To secure them: change the default admin password, update the firmware, make sure they're not reachable from the internet, use a dedicated low-privilege account for scan-to-email, turn off features you don't use, and wipe or remove the storage before the device leaves. Most of this takes under an hour per device and is often handled by your leasing company if you ask.

Why printers are a risk

  • Default passwords on the admin page let anyone on the network change settings, read the address book or redirect scans.
  • Stored data: many copiers keep scanned and printed documents on internal storage.
  • Saved credentials: scan-to-email and scan-to-folder settings often hold a mailbox or network password.
  • Old firmware with known vulnerabilities, because nobody thinks to update a printer.
  • Old protocols: some older copiers need SMBv1 or basic email authentication to scan, which can push you to weaken settings elsewhere.

The checklist

#CheckDone?
1Admin web page password changed from the default and stored in the company password manager
2Firmware updated, and automatic updates on if the device supports them; ask the leasing company to include updates in the service contract
3Not reachable from the internet: no port forwards on the router for printing or the admin page
4On the office network only, never on guest Wi-Fi (see guest Wi-Fi security)
5Scan-to-email uses a dedicated account or the method your email provider recommends, not a staff member's personal mailbox password
6Scan-to-folder uses a dedicated account with access to the scan folder only, and doesn't need SMBv1
7Unused features off: open Wi-Fi Direct, FTP, Telnet, old protocols, remote panel access
8Stored job and document retention minimized, and secure or PIN release used for sensitive printing if available
9Address book reviewed: no stale or unknown external addresses
10Device listed in your asset inventory with model, serial, firmware version and lease details
11Storage wiped or removed, with written confirmation, before the device is returned, sold or recycled (see secure disposal)

Scan-to-email without weakening email security

Many older copiers send email using basic username-and-password SMTP. As Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace move away from basic authentication for many scenarios, these setups can break or tempt people to switch off protections. Better options, depending on your provider and device:

  • The provider's recommended method for devices, such as an SMTP relay or connector restricted to your office's IP address.
  • A copier firmware update that supports modern authentication.
  • Scan-to-cloud features that save directly to OneDrive, SharePoint or Google Drive.

Ask your IT provider to choose the method, and don't reuse a real user's password on the copier. Check that your SPF record includes the copier's sending path if it sends as your domain (see SPF, DKIM and DMARC).

Questions for your copier leasing company

  • Does the device have internal storage, and is it encrypted?
  • Are firmware updates included in the service contract, and how often are they applied?
  • What happens to the storage when the lease ends? Will you provide written confirmation of the wipe or removal?
  • How does your technician access the device remotely, if at all?

Industry notes

  • Medical, dental and therapy practices: printed and scanned documents often contain PHI; treat copier storage as part of your risk assessment (see the HIPAA security risk assessment).
  • Tax preparers and financial businesses: scanned returns and IDs are customer information under the FTC Safeguards Rule (see the checklist).
  • Law firms: stored scans can include privileged documents (see the law firm checklist).

Frequently asked questions

Are office printers a security risk?

Yes. Multifunction printers have admin pages, firmware, stored scans and saved email passwords. Default passwords, old firmware and internet exposure make them an easy target.

How do I secure scan-to-email on a copier?

Use your email provider's recommended device method, such as an IP-restricted SMTP relay or connector, a firmware update with modern authentication, or scan-to-cloud, and never store a real user's password on the copier.

Do I need to wipe a copier before returning it?

Yes, if it has internal storage. Ask the leasing company to wipe or remove the drive and give you written confirmation before collection.
