Managed IT SLA template for small business: priorities, response times and scope

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

A managed IT service level agreement (SLA) defines how fast your IT provider responds to each kind of problem, when support is available, what's included in the monthly fee, and what the client is responsible for. For small business managed IT, the core is a four-level priority table with response and resolution targets, plus clear scope and exclusions.

A clear SLA prevents the two most common arguments between MSPs and clients: "why did this take so long?" and "isn't that included?". Below is a common structure with example targets. Adjust them to what you can actually staff, and have any contract reviewed by an attorney before use.

Example priority levels

PriorityDefinitionExampleResponseTarget resolution
P1 CriticalBusiness-wide outage or active security incidentEmail down for everyone; ransomware; internet down at the main office30 minutes4 hours (or continuous work until resolved)
P2 HighA major function or team can't workAccounting app down; a shared printer the whole front desk uses2 hours1 business day
P3 NormalOne user affected, workaround existsOne laptop slow; Outlook search not working1 business day3 business days
P4 LowRequest, question or planned changeNew user setup; software install; how-to question2 business days5 business days, or as scheduled

Define response precisely: a technician acknowledging the ticket and starting work, not an automated email. Define resolution as a target, not a guarantee, since some fixes depend on vendors.

Sections to include

  1. Parties, term and renewal. Start date, length (often 12 months), auto-renewal and notice period.
  2. Support hours. Business hours and time zone, after-hours coverage for P1 only or for all priorities, holiday coverage, and any after-hours rates.
  3. How to request support. Portal, email and phone, and which one to use for P1 (always phone). Requests outside these channels don't start the SLA clock.
  4. Included services. Helpdesk, patching, monitoring, backup management, antivirus/EDR, user adds and removals, vendor liaison, and a set number of on-site visits.
  5. Excluded services. Projects (migrations, new offices, new servers), hardware and software purchases, training, recovery from incidents caused by unsupported systems, and anything billed hourly.
  6. Client responsibilities. Name an authorized contact, keep supported hardware and software, follow security policies, approve security changes promptly, and pay for required licenses.
  7. Security responsibilities. Who manages MFA, backups, admin accounts and incident response, and who notifies the insurer. Grey areas here cause disputes after an incident.
  8. Reporting and reviews. Monthly ticket and SLA reports, and quarterly business reviews.
  9. Fees and changes. Per-user or per-device pricing, how adding users changes the fee, annual price adjustments, and late payment terms.
  10. Termination and offboarding. Notice period, handover of credentials and documentation, and removal of the MSP's tools and access.

Sample wording you can adapt

Priority 1 issues must be reported by phone to [number]. The Provider will respond within 30 minutes during Support Hours and within 60 minutes outside Support Hours, and will work continuously until the issue is resolved or downgraded with the Client's agreement.
Response targets apply only to requests submitted through the support portal, support email or support phone line. Targets are measured during Support Hours unless stated otherwise and exclude time spent waiting on the Client or third-party vendors.

Mistakes that cause SLA disputes

  • No definition of "critical". Without one, every ticket is critical.
  • Unlimited "all IT" scope. Spell out projects as excluded, or your flat fee covers a server migration.
  • Security left vague. If the client's insurer asks who manages backups and MFA, the SLA should answer it.
  • Response times you can't staff. A 15-minute response promise from a two-person MSP is a liability, not a selling point.

Frequently asked questions

What is a typical P1 response time for an MSP?

Many small-business MSPs target 15 to 60 minutes for critical issues, depending on price and staffing.

What response times should a managed IT SLA include?

A common structure is 30 minutes for critical outages, 2 hours for high priority, 1 business day for normal issues and 2 business days for low-priority requests, with resolution targets for each. Set targets you can actually staff.

What should be excluded from a managed IT agreement?

Commonly: projects such as migrations or new offices, hardware and software purchases, training, and work on unsupported systems. List exclusions explicitly to avoid disputes.

What is the difference between response time and resolution time?

Response time is how quickly a technician acknowledges the issue and starts work. Resolution time is a target for fixing it. SLAs usually guarantee response and set resolution as a target.
