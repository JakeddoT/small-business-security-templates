MFA rollout plan for small business: 5 phases and a staff email template

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

Roll out multi-factor authentication in five phases: admin accounts first, then email for everyone, then remote access, then finance and customer-data apps, then everything else. A phased rollout over two to four weeks avoids locking people out, keeps the helpdesk manageable and gets the highest-risk accounts protected in the first few days.

MFA is the single control insurers ask about most (see cyber insurance MFA requirements), and it blocks the large majority of password-based account takeovers. The hard part isn't turning it on; it's getting every person enrolled without disruption.

Before you start (week 0)

  • Pick the method. An authenticator app with number matching (Microsoft Authenticator, Google Authenticator, Duo) for most staff; security keys or passkeys for admins if you can. Avoid SMS where possible.
  • List every account and app staff sign in to, including shared mailboxes and service accounts.
  • Decide on phones. If staff use personal phones for the authenticator app, say so in your BYOD policy. Have a few security keys ready for anyone who can't or won't use a personal phone.
  • Create break-glass access. Keep one emergency admin account with a long password and a security key, stored securely, so a misconfiguration can't lock everyone out.

The 5 phases

PhaseScopeWhenWatch for
1. Admin and IT accountsMicrosoft 365 / Google admins, firewall, backup console, registrar, your MSP's accountsDays 1–2Old admin accounts nobody uses: disable them
2. Email for all usersEvery mailbox, including the owner and part-timersWeek 1Shared logins and old phones with legacy mail apps
3. VPN and remote accessVPN, remote desktop gateways, remote-support toolsWeek 2Vendors with standing access
4. Finance and customer-data appsBanking, payroll, accounting, CRM, practice management, file sharingWeeks 2–3Apps with their own logins, not single sign-on
5. Everything elseSocial media, vendor portals, domain and web hosting, anything that supports MFAWeeks 3–4Accounts tied to one person's phone

In Microsoft 365, turning on Security defaults (or a Conditional Access policy if you have Entra ID P1) handles phases 1–2 for Microsoft sign-ins. In Google Workspace, use Admin console → Security → Authentication → 2-Step Verification, with an enrollment deadline before enforcement.

Staff announcement email template

Subject: Action needed: set up multi-factor authentication by [DATE]

Hi everyone,

To protect our business, our customers and our insurance coverage, we're turning on multi-factor authentication (MFA) for [SYSTEM, e.g. email] on [DATE]. After that date, signing in will need your password plus a quick approval on your phone.

What to do before [DATE] (about 5 minutes):

  1. Install [APP] from the App Store or Google Play.
  2. Follow the steps at [LINK / attached guide].
  3. Test it by signing out and back in.

Need help? [IT CONTACT] will be at [LOCATION] on [DATE/TIME], or reply to this email.

One important rule: never approve a sign-in prompt you didn't start. If you get one, deny it and tell [IT CONTACT] right away; it means someone has your password.

Thanks,
[NAME]

An empty exceptions list is the goal. Review it before every insurance renewal; an exception is often the difference between a "yes" and an honest "partly" on the application.

Common rollout problems

  • New phone, lost access. Explain how to move the authenticator to a new phone, and give IT a way to reset MFA after verifying the person's identity.
  • MFA fatigue. Attackers spam prompts hoping someone taps Approve. Number matching stops most of this; staff training handles the rest.
  • Legacy sign-in still on. Old email protocols can bypass MFA. Make sure they're blocked.

Frequently asked questions

How long does an MFA rollout take for a small business?

Most small businesses can complete a phased rollout in two to four weeks.

What is MFA fatigue?

An attack where users are spammed with MFA prompts until they approve one. Train staff never to approve prompts they did not start.

How long does it take to roll out MFA in a small business?

For most small businesses, two to four weeks in phases: admins in the first days, email in week one, then remote access, finance apps and everything else.

What if an employee won't use their personal phone for MFA?

Offer a hardware security key instead. Keep a few on hand, and document the approach in your BYOD or MFA policy.

Which accounts should get MFA first?

Admin accounts first (Microsoft 365 or Google admins, firewall, backup console, domain registrar), then email for every user, then remote access.
