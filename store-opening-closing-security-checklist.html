Store opening and closing security checklist for retail and restaurants

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

At opening, check that every card terminal is the right one and untouched, POS logins are individual, and nothing was left connected overnight. At closing, lock away handhelds and spares, sign out of every POS and back-office account, and secure the office PC. Five minutes at each end of the day prevents most skimming, after-hours misuse and "who was logged in?" problems.

Print the two lists below, keep them at the register, and have the opening and closing manager initial them each day.

Opening checklist (5 minutes)

#Check✔
1Every card terminal and PIN pad is present, in its usual place, and its serial number matches the inventory☐
2Quick tamper check on each terminal: card slot, keypad, cables and seals (2-minute skimmer check)☐
3No unfamiliar devices plugged into registers, network ports or the router☐
4Each staff member signs in to the POS with their own login; no shared manager code left logged in☐
5Back-office PC locked until needed; screen lock on☐
6Guest Wi-Fi is the only network customers can see or use (guest Wi-Fi setup)☐
7Any after-hours alerts (alarm, camera, POS or bank notices) reviewed☐

Closing checklist (5 minutes)

#Check✔
1All staff signed out of the POS and any back-office apps☐
2Handheld and spare terminals counted and locked away☐
3Back-office PC locked or shut down; no cards, receipts or reports left out☐
4Paper with card or customer data shredded, not binned☐
5Cash office and network/router closet locked☐
6Note anything unusual today: a visitor who asked to "check" a terminal, a strange call, a device that acted oddly☐

Sign-off log

DateOpening (initials, time)Closing (initials, time)Issues
11-18JM 08:52AK 21:14None
11-19JM 08:50AK 21:20Caller claiming to be "processor support" asked for terminal ID; refused, reported to manager

Keep the log with your skimmer inspection log. If your processor or insurer ever asks how you protect terminals, the log is your answer.

Weekly and monthly extras

  • Weekly: fuller terminal inspection (underneath, behind and inside any mounts), and a check that departed staff no longer have POS logins.
  • Monthly: change the guest Wi-Fi password if it's posted publicly, review who has manager or refund permissions, and check POS and router updates.
  • Yearly: complete your PCI self-assessment questionnaire (see the PCI checklist) and retrain staff, logging it in a training log.

The rule staff must know

Nobody services, replaces or "updates" a card terminal, and nobody plugs anything into a register, unless the manager has confirmed it with our POS provider on a number we already have.

Fake technicians and phone "support" are a common way terminals get swapped. If something looks wrong, stop using the terminal, don't remove anything, and call the manager and the payment processor.

Frequently asked questions

What should a store closing security checklist include?

Signing everyone out of the POS, locking away handhelds and spare terminals, securing the back-office PC, shredding paper with card data, locking the cash office and network closet, and noting anything unusual.

How often should retail staff check card terminals?

A quick check at opening every day, with a fuller inspection weekly. Log each check with the date and initials.

What should staff do if someone asks to service a card terminal?

Refuse until the manager confirms the visit with the POS provider on a known phone number. Fake technicians are a common way terminals get tampered with or swapped.
