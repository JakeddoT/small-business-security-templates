FTC Safeguards Rule for auto dealers: a practical checklist

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-22

Auto dealers that arrange or extend financing or leasing are generally treated as financial institutions under the FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314), so they need a written information security program with a Qualified Individual in charge, a risk assessment, access controls, MFA, encryption of customer information, staff training, oversight of service providers, an incident response plan, and reporting of certain breaches to the FTC. In a dealership, that means protecting credit applications, deal jackets, copies of driver's licenses, the dealer management system (DMS) and everything F&I touches.

This page is general information, not legal advice. Your dealer association, compliance consultant or lawyer can confirm how the rule applies to your store. For the rule in general, see the FTC Safeguards Rule checklist.

The checklist

TestingContinuous monitoring, or annual penetration testing and vulnerability scans at least every six months
Annual reportThe Qualified Individual reports in writing to the owner or board at least yearly

Under the rule, businesses that maintain customer information on fewer than 5,000 consumers are exempt from some of the written requirements, such as the written risk assessment, the written incident response plan, the annual report and certain testing. Most of the core safeguards still apply. Check the exemption carefully before relying on it.

Breach reporting

Since May 2024, the rule has required financial institutions to notify the FTC within 30 days of discovering a "notification event" involving unencrypted information of at least 500 consumers. State breach laws may also require notice to customers and state authorities. Put both in your incident plan.

Dealership habits that cause trouble

  • Shared DMS or lender portal logins in the sales tower or F&I office.
  • Credit applications sent as email attachments to and from customers.
  • Photos of licenses and documents on salespeople's personal phones. Use the CRM or DMS capture tools instead and set a rule.
  • Deal jackets in open filing or stacked in an unlocked office.
  • Vendor remote access that's always on with a shared password.
  • Wire and payoff fraud: confirm any change to lender, floor plan or payoff bank details by phone on a known number (see bank detail change verification).

Frequently asked questions

Does the FTC Safeguards Rule apply to car dealerships?

Dealers that arrange or extend financing or leasing are generally covered as financial institutions under the FTC Safeguards Rule, so they need a written information security program and the safeguards the rule lists.

What does the Safeguards Rule require of auto dealers?

A Qualified Individual, a written risk assessment, access controls, a data inventory, encryption, MFA, secure disposal, monitoring, training, service provider oversight, an incident response plan, testing, an annual report and FTC breach reporting, with some written requirements exempt for businesses with fewer than 5,000 consumers.

When must a dealer report a breach to the FTC?

Within 30 days of discovering a notification event involving unencrypted information of at least 500 consumers. State breach laws may add separate requirements.
