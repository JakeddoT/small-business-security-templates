HIPAA security checklist for therapists in private practice

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-22

For a solo or small therapy practice, HIPAA security comes down to: use an EHR and telehealth platform that will sign a business associate agreement (BAA), turn on MFA for them and your email, encrypt and lock the laptop and phone you use for client work, don't keep client details in personal texts, email or notes apps, keep psychotherapy notes separate and protected, do a yearly risk assessment, and know what to do if a device is lost or a message goes to the wrong person. The rules apply to you as a covered entity if you conduct certain transactions electronically, such as billing insurance, which most practices that take insurance do.

This page is general information, not legal advice. For anything borderline, talk to a healthcare lawyer or your professional association.

The checklist

AreaWhat to doDone?
EHR / practice managementVendor signs a BAA; MFA on; your own login, not shared with a biller or assistant
TelehealthA platform that offers a BAA and is designed for healthcare. The pandemic-era enforcement flexibility for non-compliant video tools ended in 2023
EmailA business email account with MFA, under a BAA if you send client information by email; send sensitive details through the EHR portal instead where possible
TextingKeep appointment reminders minimal; don't discuss clinical details in personal text apps; use the EHR's messaging if it has one
LaptopFull-disk encryption (BitLocker or FileVault) on, automatic updates, screen lock, no shared family use (see checking BitLocker)
PhonePasscode, encryption, remote wipe, no client details in photos or notes apps
PasswordsUnique passwords in a password manager (see the password manager rollout)
PaperLocked cabinet; shredding, not recycling
Psychotherapy notesKept separate from the general record where your EHR supports it, with tighter access
Business associatesBAAs with EHR, telehealth, billing service, email provider, cloud storage and IT support (see the BAA checklist)
Risk assessmentWritten, reviewed at least yearly and when something changes (see the security risk assessment)
BackupsEHR data backed up by the vendor; any local files backed up and restorable
Home officeSessions where you can't be overheard; screen not visible to others (see the home office checklist)

Group practices and shared offices

  • Each clinician and staff member has their own EHR login with access to only their clients, plus what supervisors need.
  • Shared front-desk computers lock automatically and nobody stays signed in as someone else.
  • When a clinician leaves, disable their accounts the same day and plan the transfer of their records and client communications (see the offboarding checklist).
  • Interns and contractors sign the same policies and complete the same training (see the training log).

Common situations

SituationWhat to do
Laptop or phone lost or stolenRemote wipe if possible, change passwords, and document whether encryption was on; an encrypted device is usually not a reportable breach (see lost laptop steps and the breach risk assessment)
Email or message sent to the wrong personAsk the recipient to delete it, document it, and assess it with the four-factor risk assessment (see sent email to the wrong person)
Email account hackedSecure the account, check what was accessed, and assess it as a possible breach (see the Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace checklist)
A client wants to communicate by regular email or textExplain the risks, follow your policy and document their preference

A one-hour yearly review

  1. List every app and device that holds client information.
  2. Confirm you have a BAA for each vendor that handles it.
  3. Confirm MFA, encryption and updates on everything.
  4. Update your risk assessment and note what changed.
  5. Do a short training refresher on phishing and misdirected messages, and log it.

Frequently asked questions

Does HIPAA apply to therapists in private practice?

HIPAA applies to therapists who are covered entities, which generally includes those who conduct certain transactions electronically, such as billing insurance. Many cash-only practices also follow it as good practice; check your situation with your professional association or a lawyer.

Can therapists use any video platform for telehealth?

The pandemic-era enforcement flexibility for non-compliant video tools ended in 2023. Use a telehealth platform designed for healthcare that will sign a business associate agreement.

Is a lost encrypted laptop a HIPAA breach for a therapist?

Generally not, if the encryption met HHS guidance and the password wasn't compromised, because the information isn't considered unsecured. Document the evidence that encryption was on.
