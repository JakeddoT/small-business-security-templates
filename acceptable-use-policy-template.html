Acceptable use policy template: what to include

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

An acceptable use policy (AUP) sets the rules for company computers, accounts, networks, and data. It protects the business and gives employees clear expectations.

Sections to include

  1. Purpose and scope: employees, contractors, and personal devices that access company data.
  2. Acceptable use: business purposes, keep credentials private, lock screens, report incidents within 24 hours.
  3. Prohibited use: unapproved software, disabling security tools, offensive material, copying data to personal accounts, unauthorized access.
  4. Monitoring: state that company systems may be monitored, subject to applicable law.
  5. Enforcement: consequences for violations.
  6. Acknowledgment: a signature line for every employee.

Tips

Keep it to one or two pages, have every new hire sign it, and re-acknowledge yearly. Pair it with a password and MFA policy.

Frequently asked questions

Do employees need to sign an acceptable use policy?

It is best practice to collect a signed acknowledgment at hire and annually, as evidence that employees were informed.

What is prohibited in a typical acceptable use policy?

Installing unapproved software, disabling security tools, sharing credentials, copying company data to personal storage, and accessing systems without authorization.
