Security awareness training log template

By Jake Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

Cyber insurance applications, client security questionnaires, HIPAA, and the FTC Safeguards Rule all ask the same thing: can you prove your staff were trained? Running training is only half of it. You also need a dated record. Here is what a simple training log should capture, and what a good small business program looks like.

What to record for every session

  • Date and topic (for example, "Phishing and invoice fraud, 30 min")
  • Format: live session, video course, phishing simulation, or written policy acknowledgment
  • Attendee name and role
  • Completion confirmation: a signature, quiz score, or platform export
  • Trainer or provider
  • Next due date

A minimum program for a small business

  1. At hire: security basics plus signing the acceptable use policy.
  2. Every year: a 30 to 45 minute refresher on phishing, passwords and MFA, payment fraud, and how to report problems.
  3. Quarterly (optional): a simulated phishing email, with short follow-up training for anyone who clicks.
  4. After an incident: a short lesson on what happened, without naming or blaming anyone.

Keep logs for at least as long as your insurer or regulator might ask about them. Six years is a common conservative choice for healthcare.

Frequently asked questions

How often should employees get security awareness training?

At hire and at least once a year, with a short refresher after any incident. Many insurers ask whether training is annual.

Does security awareness training need to be documented?

Yes. Insurers, auditors, and regulators generally expect a dated record of who completed training and when.

Can a small business run its own security training?

Yes. A 30-minute live session covering phishing, MFA, payment fraud, and reporting counts if it is documented.
