Medical and dental office security checklist

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

Small practices hold very sensitive data, usually without a dedicated IT person. These are the basics to put in place first.

  1. Know where patient data lives: EHR, email, file shares, laptops, phones, scanners, fax, paper.
  2. Unique logins for everyone. No shared front-desk accounts.
  3. MFA on email and remote access.
  4. Encrypt laptops and phones.
  5. Track vendors that touch patient data and their agreement dates.
  6. Tested backups with one copy offline or immutable.
  7. Train staff at hire and yearly, and document it.
  8. Have a breach checklist ready. Notification rules have deadlines; contact counsel promptly.
  9. Do a risk assessment yearly and after major changes.

Frequently asked questions

Do small medical offices need a security risk assessment?

HIPAA requires covered entities to conduct a risk analysis. A qualified compliance professional can confirm what applies to your practice.

Can front-desk staff share one login?

Shared logins make it impossible to know who accessed records. Each workforce member should have a unique login.
