Restaurant cybersecurity checklist

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

A restaurant's biggest cyber risks are the card terminals and POS, the Wi-Fi that guests and staff share, the online ordering, delivery and reservation accounts, shared staff logins, and phishing aimed at the owner or manager. The essentials: keep the POS and card readers on their own network away from guest Wi-Fi, check card readers for tampering, give each staff member their own POS login, turn on MFA for every online account, and control who your POS vendor lets in remotely. Most of it costs nothing and can be done in an afternoon.

Restaurants are attractive targets because they handle lots of card payments, have high staff turnover, rely on shared devices and often run on a mix of vendor systems that nobody owns end to end. Card data rules (PCI DSS) apply to any business that accepts card payments, and your processor or POS provider will usually ask you to confirm your setup each year (see the PCI compliance checklist).

The checklist

POS and card payments

CheckDone?
POS terminals and card readers are on a separate network from guest Wi-Fi
Card readers inspected for skimmers and tampering, ideally every shift (see how to check for skimmers)
Every staff member has their own POS login or PIN; no shared manager code
Refunds, voids and discounts need a manager login, and the reports are reviewed weekly
Default passwords changed on the POS back office, router and any kitchen display system
Your POS vendor's remote access is limited: you know which tool they use, and it uses MFA
POS back-office computer isn't used for personal browsing or email
Card numbers are never written down or stored, for example for phone orders

Wi-Fi and network

CheckDone?
Guest Wi-Fi is separate from staff and POS networks (see guest Wi-Fi security)
Staff Wi-Fi password changed when people leave, and not printed on the menu or wall
Router admin password changed from the default, and router firmware kept updated
Network equipment is somewhere customers and delivery drivers can't reach

Online ordering, delivery and reservations

CheckDone?
MFA on delivery platforms, online ordering, reservation system, payment processor dashboard and social media
Logins kept in a password manager, not a sticky note by the delivery tablet (see password manager rollout)
Bank account and payout details on each platform checked monthly, and alerts turned on for changes
Ex-staff removed from every platform on their last day
QR codes on tables and menus checked for stickers placed over them (see QR code phishing)

Staff, email and scams

CheckDone?
MFA on the owner's and managers' email
Staff know that calls claiming to be the POS company, processor or a delivery platform asking for codes or remote access are often scams; call back on the known number
A rule that gift card purchases are never made because of an email or text (see the gift card scam)
Supplier bank detail changes verified by phone on a known number (see bank detail change verification)
Payroll direct deposit changes verified in person (see the direct deposit scam)

Backups and recovery

CheckDone?
Menu, recipes, schedules and accounting data backed up, with a restore tested (see backup restore test)
A fallback for taking payments if the POS or internet is down, agreed with your processor
The POS vendor's support number and your account number written down somewhere offline

Opening and closing routine

The easiest way to keep this going is to add a few items to the opening and closing routine your managers already follow: check card readers, check table QR codes, lock the office and network cabinet, sign out of the back-office computer. See the opening and closing security checklist.

When staff leave

High turnover is normal in hospitality, so make offboarding quick: disable their POS login, remove them from delivery and ordering platforms, the schedule app and any shared email, and change the staff Wi-Fi and any shared passwords they knew (see the offboarding checklist).

If something goes wrong

  • Suspected skimmer or card data issue: stop using the device, don't wipe it, and call your processor.
  • Delivery or ordering account taken over: reset the password, check payout details, and contact the platform.
  • Ransomware on the back-office computer: disconnect it and follow the first-hour steps.

Frequently asked questions

What are the biggest cybersecurity risks for restaurants?

Card readers and POS systems, shared Wi-Fi, online ordering and delivery accounts without MFA, shared staff logins, and phishing or phone scams aimed at managers.

Should restaurant guest Wi-Fi be separate from the POS?

Yes. Keep POS terminals and card readers on their own network, separate from the Wi-Fi guests and staff phones use.

Does PCI DSS apply to small restaurants?

PCI DSS applies to any business that accepts card payments. Your processor or POS provider usually tells you which self-assessment to complete each year.
