MSP client onboarding checklist: what to collect in the first week

By William Todd, IT support and cybersecurity engineer · Updated 2026-11-21

In the first week with a new managed IT client, collect eight things: business context, every admin credential, a device inventory, network details, licenses and renewals, a security baseline, a signed SLA, and an agreed maintenance rhythm. Miss one and you'll be hunting for it during the first outage, usually the registrar login or the backup console.

This checklist is written for small MSPs and solo IT consultants taking on clients with 5–50 users. Each item lists what to collect and where it typically hides.

The 8-part onboarding checklist

1. Business context (day 1 meeting)

  • Headcount, locations, remote and hybrid staff, business hours, after-hours needs
  • Line-of-business apps and who the vendor contact is for each
  • Compliance or contractual drivers: HIPAA, PCI, FTC Safeguards Rule, cyber insurance requirements, client security questionnaires
  • Decision-maker, billing contact, and who can authorize changes or emergency spend

2. Admin access (days 1–3)

  • Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace global admin; set up your own named admin account with MFA rather than using the owner's login
  • Domain registrar and DNS host (often a forgotten account in a former employee's name)
  • Firewall, router, switches and Wi-Fi controller
  • Backup console, antivirus or EDR console, and any RMM left by a previous provider
  • Line-of-business apps, ISP portal, phone system, printers and copier vendor
  • Store everything in your password manager immediately, never in email or a shared document

4. Network details

  • ISP, account number, support phone, circuit type, static IPs
  • Firewall model, firmware version, and every inbound rule or port forward (look for exposed RDP; see how to check)
  • Wi-Fi networks, guest isolation, VLANs and subnets
  • DNS records: MX, SPF, DKIM, DMARC; note anything missing

5. Licenses and renewals

  • Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace licenses and who's assigned what (look for paid licenses on departed users)
  • Domain and SSL renewal dates, warranty and support contracts, line-of-business app renewals
  • Put every renewal date in a shared calendar with 60-day reminders

6. Security baseline

  • MFA coverage, password manager use, admin account count
  • Backups: what's backed up, where, how often, and when a restore was last tested
  • Written policies, incident response plan, and staff training records
  • Cyber insurance policy and the last questionnaire answers. If the client answered "yes" to MFA everywhere and it isn't true, that's your first project. See the questionnaire guide.

Every gap becomes a line in your first 90-day project plan, and that plan is your first quarterly business review.

7. An agreed SLA

Priority levels, response targets, support hours, what's included and excluded. See the managed IT SLA template.

8. A maintenance rhythm

  • Monthly: patches, backup checks, AV and EDR alerts, license and account review
  • Quarterly: business review with the owner, restore test, admin account review
  • Yearly: policy review, tabletop exercise, insurance renewal prep

Onboarding mistakes that cost MSPs later

  • Inheriting the previous provider's access. Revoke the old MSP's accounts and remote tools on day one, with the client's written approval.
  • Using the owner's credentials. Always create named admin accounts; it protects you if something goes wrong.
  • No written baseline. Document what you found on day one, so you're not blamed later for problems you inherited.
  • Skipping DNS. Losing access to the registrar is one of the hardest problems to recover from.

Frequently asked questions

What should an MSP collect during client onboarding?

Business context, admin access, device and network inventory, licenses, a security baseline, and an agreed SLA.

How do MSPs show value to clients?

Monthly maintenance reports and quarterly business reviews that track tickets, security posture, and recommendations.

What should an MSP collect when onboarding a new client?

Business context, all admin credentials (stored in a password manager), a device inventory, network details including firewall rules and DNS, licenses and renewal dates, a security baseline, a signed SLA and an agreed maintenance schedule.

How long does MSP client onboarding take?

For a 5–50 user small business, plan on about one to two weeks to collect access, inventory and a security baseline, followed by a 90-day plan to close the gaps you find.

What should an MSP do about the previous IT provider's access?

With the client's written approval, revoke the previous provider's accounts, remote access tools and RMM agents on day one, and change any shared admin passwords they knew.
